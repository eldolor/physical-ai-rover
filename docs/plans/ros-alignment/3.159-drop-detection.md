# 3.159 No fall at a stair: a drop ahead stops the robot

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.159; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** (the user, 2026-11-06: features of a robot vacuum (Dreame) worth leveraging, for full autonomy; 3.158) Every robot vacuum stops at a stair edge with downward cliff
sensors. This robot has nothing like it: `robot/safety.py` vets a move
against the depth-grid cone, the lidar's swept corridor and 3.74's floor
band, all of which look for *things*, not for *missing floor*. The lidar
scans at its own height and sees a stairwell as open space. The user's
house has a basement and a stair hall (3.122's walks), so for a robot sent
out alone a drop is the likeliest serious accident. Priority: high, after
the dead-brain fixes (3.145-3.147).

**Scope.**
* A drop check in series with the cone, corridor and floor band, by their
  rules (CLAUDE.md rule 4 and section 6 "Safety"): the floor ahead, from the
  OAK-D's depth, lower than the floor plane by more than a threshold, or not
  seen where floor should be, refuses FORWARD (and the swept turn that
  would carry the chassis over it) with a named reason. An unusable zone is
  never clear (M3).
* Blind range: what the camera cannot see under the bumper (3.74's known
  gap) is stated, with the margin it needs.
* A simulated stairwell (a drop cell type in the grid world and the
  renderer's depth) so it is judged on ground truth in the sweeps
  (`tests/footprint_sweep.py`'s way), never on the readings the veto uses.
* nav2: the drop marked into the costmap (keepout) so plans avoid it, not
  only the veto.
* On the Rover: whether the camera's floor view suffices, or cheap downward
  IR sensors on the board's spare inputs are needed (decided by step C's
  numbers).

## Design (3.159.A, 2026-11-07)

What it rests on: 3.74's floor band (a `floor` block inside
`get_depth_grid()`, judged by `_corridor_room` and put in series in
`forward_clearance()`), M3 (an unusable zone never enters a comparison),
3.19's pivot guard (a turn refused only when it closes on something),
3.141's frozen references (`tests/pivot_reference.py`,
`tests/vet_reference.py`) and the wedge watch's keepout path (3.83/3.85).
Today nothing sees missing floor: the lidar scans at 12 cm and sees a
stairwell as open space, the cone and the band look for things, and the
simulator's camera has no floor plane at all.

**D1. What the sensor reports: a seen-floor distance per column.** A new
optional `drop` block in the depth grid, beside `floor`, with the same
geometry fields (`cols`, `fov_deg`, `pan_deg`, `origin_x_cm`, `near_cm`,
`far_cm`) and one zone per column:
* `ZONE_RANGE` with `distance_cm` = how far along that column the floor is
  seen continuously, i.e. where it ends: a point lower than the floor
  plane by more than `DROP_CM` (5 cm), or no return where floor should
  be (a stairwell into the dark, or a lip that hides the lower floor);
* `NO_TARGET`: floor seen out to `far_cm` (no edge in range);
* `UNUSABLE`: the column could not be read.
A rise of up to 3 cm (a rug, a door threshold) counts as floor; more is
an object, the cone's and the band's. On the car the block is computed
from the OAK-D's depth image with the camera's height and pitch from the
xacro (step C); in the simulator from the grid (D5).

**D2. The rule: an edge is a wall at the end of seen floor, and unseen is
never clear.** `drop_clearance(grid)` turns each `ZONE_RANGE` edge into a
body-frame point and judges it with `_corridor_room` exactly as the band
judges its returns, so it joins `forward_clearance()`'s `_nearer(...)`
in series with the cone, the corridor and the band, aged the same way,
reasons `drop_edge`, `drop_unseen`, `no_drop_sensor`. What makes it
stricter than the band (M3, both ways):
* `NO_TARGET` is clear only inside the path half-angle, as the band's is.
* `UNUSABLE` and the blind range are **not** clear and **not** a distance:
  they fall back to the **floor memory** (D3). Forward is allowed only as
  far as floor has been seen, now or remembered. A dropout therefore does
  not stop the robot on floor it has just seen, and does not let it drive
  onto floor it never saw.
* A backend that publishes no `drop` block at all (the car today, with no
  OAK-D driver) reads `no_drop_sensor`: the rule is off and says so in
  `/health`'s description list (not its verdict, M5), as `no_floor_band`
  does. `drop_guard_required` (config, default off) refuses autonomy
  without the block; step C turns it on for the Rover once the sensor is
  proven. A block that is present but entirely unusable is the first
  case: memory only.

**D3. Floor memory covers the blind range, astern and turns.** Seen floor
is kept as cells (5 cm) in the odometry frame for the last 3 m travelled
or 60 s, whichever is shorter, shrunk by 2% of the distance travelled
since it was seen (odometry drift); drop edges are kept the same way.
At start the robot's own outline plus 5 cm is floor (it stands on it).
* **Blind range.** The camera reads floor from about 14 cm past the
  bumper (MinZ 20 cm from a lens 6.5 cm ahead of `base_link`; 3.74). The
  strip under the bumper is covered by memory: it was seen when it was
  farther away. Driving straight ahead it always was; after a turn it
  may not have been, and then forward waits for memory or a reading.
* **Astern.** Nothing looks behind. `reverse_clearance()` gains the same
  rule against memory only: reverse over floor seen or driven, else
  `drop_unseen_astern`.
* **The swept turn.** A new `drop_pivot_blocked()`, in series beside 3.19's
  guard and not inside it (the frozen `_PivotRows` and its references stay
  byte-identical): a turn is refused when the chassis' swept outline would
  enter a remembered edge cell, or a never-seen cell, and the turn closes
  on it; a turn that opens the gap is never touched (3.19's rule). The
  corners reach 4.5 cm past the half-length in a pivot, so this matters
  only within a hand's width of an edge.

**D4. Speed and stopping.** The required clearance stays
`required_clearance_cm(v)` (20 cm or the stop distance: 48 cm at
0.6 m/s with today's placeholder deceleration). Seen floor must reach at
least that far, so speed is capped where floor is seen short; floor is
read out to `far_cm` = 100 cm in the simulator, and step C measures how
far the OAK-D reads floor at 10 cm height (grazing incidence) before any
speed above nav2's 0.2 m/s is allowed near an edge.

**D5. The simulator's stairwell.** New cell characters in
`sim/grid_world.py`:
* `v`, a drop (a stairwell or a sunken floor, 18 cm down): passable to the
  lidar and nav2 (they see open space, as on the car), not solid, never
  floor for the robot;
* `r`, a rug (1.5 cm up) and `t`, a door threshold strip (1.5 cm up):
  floor for everything, there to cost false stops.
The renderer computes the `drop` block per column by stepping 1 cm along
the ray from `near_cm` to `far_cm` over cell heights, with S5's noise and
dropout from its own seeded stream, and a switch for what lies past an
edge: `no_return` or `lower_floor` (both are judged; the car may show
either). Existing houses gain no new cells, so their fingerprints
(`tests/test_sim_fingerprint.py`) are unchanged. New maps:
`scaled_house_stairs` and `home_first_floor_stairs` (a stair hall opening
off the hall, the basement's stairs) plus rug and threshold cells in all
four houses' `_floors` variants.
**Ground truth** (`tests/footprint_sweep.py`'s way, sharing no code with
the rule): a **fall** is the chassis centre over a `v` cell; an
**overhang** is any part of the true chassis rectangle over a `v` cell;
the **edge gap** is the true distance from the outline to the nearest
`v` cell at rest. `grid_world.translate()` lets the body overhang (it does
not stop at an edge, as the car would not); a fall ends the run.

**D6. The costmap mark.** A drop edge the robot has seen is marked into
nav2's keepout so plans go around it, not only into the veto. The robot
server owns it (the rule and memory live there): seen edge cells become
discs (r 0.10 m) through the existing `world_model.set_keepout()` ->
bridge `POST /nav/keepout`, with a `kind: drop` the bridge keeps apart
from the wedge spots: no TTL within the session, not cleared at a
mission's end (3.76 K5.3 clears wedge spots only), its own cap (64 discs).
Saving drop marks with the map (3.50.D) is step D, not B: B's marks last
for the session.

**D7. Safety unchanged elsewhere.** No new movement path; the rule is
inside `forward_clearance()`/`reverse_clearance()`, so the wheel loop,
verbs, creeps and nav2's twists (which reach the wheels through
`vet_wheel_velocity`) all get it. 3.145 holds `robot/server.py` and
`robot/interface.py`: B keeps the `drop` block a sim-side field read by
`safety.py` (the interface's docstring gains it in a later step), and the
keepout wiring in `robot/server.py` waits for 3.145 if `overlap` shows a
clash (then D6 is built as B2 after it).

## Criteria (3.159.B; written 2026-11-07, before any code or measurement)

Harnesses, as CLAUDE.md section 7 asks:
* **Approach sweep** (`tests/footprint_sweep.py`'s loop: the wheel loop's
  `vet_wheel_velocity` then `advance`, 0.05 s steps): a straight edge
  across the path at 30-150 cm, approach angles 0-70 deg, an outside
  corner and a 0.9 m stairwell opening; 300 placements x speeds
  {0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 m/s} x {`no_return`, `lower_floor`} = 2,400 runs,
  exact sensor, and again under S5 noise (sigma 3 cm, dropout 0.2).
* **Turn and astern sweep:** 300 pivots each way with the outline 1-15 cm
  from an edge; 300 reverses toward an edge the robot drove away from and
  300 toward one it never saw.
* **Flat-floor sweep** (3.74 B2a's): the four houses with rugs and
  thresholds and no drops, 1,920 runs, exact and S5.
* **Missions in process:** 20 tiered and 20 survey missions on each
  stairs house, and the same starts with the stairwell walled off.
* **Live:** one mission on `home_first_floor_stairs` through the brain's
  HTTP API, nav2 on, under the desktop's exclusive lock.

D1-D3 are the stair half, D5 and D6 the progress half: a rule that stops
everywhere passes D1 and fails D5. 3.74's own noisy false-stop bar failed
at 10.2% (per-zone noise); D5's 2% is set knowing that, and memory is the
design's answer to it; a miss is recorded as missed. Step C repeats D1-D3
and D5 on the Rover at a real stair edge with the user present, and
decides IR cliff sensors from them.

### Amendment 1 (2026-11-07, written before any B measurement)

Building B in the simulator settled these; each changes a harness or a
bar's reach, never a bar's number.

1. **Rugs and thresholds are not modelled.** The grid simulator's drop
   block reads what a cell is, not how high it is, so a 1.5 cm rise would
   be floor by construction: it would cost nothing here and prove
   nothing. D5's flat-floor sweep runs the four houses as they are, with
   the drop block on; rugs and thresholds are judged on the Rover (C).
2. **One past-edge mode.** `no_return` and `lower_floor` give the same
   block in a 2-D world (the floor ends at the lip). The approach sweep is
   300 placements x 4 speeds = 1,200 runs, exact and again under S5.
3. **Memory forgets by travel only** (3 m of path, pruned once it holds
   10 m^2), not after 60 s: dead reckoning drifts with distance, and a
   robot that waits must keep the strip under its bumper. No drift
   shrink: the simulator's odometry is exact unless drift is asked for.
4. **The creep margin.** Room before an edge or unseen floor is reported
   less (20 - 3) cm, so a creep (3.44's 3 cm bar) stops at the full 20 cm
   from a drop, not at 3 cm. At walking speed the robot so stops ~37 cm
   from an edge.
5. **The turn sweep is judged on truth.** A rectangle's corners swing out
   whichever way it turns, so "a turn away from an edge" is defined by the
   truth: a pivot whose true gap over its first 5 degrees does not shrink.
   D4's first bar is those refused 0 times; D1/D2 cover the rest. A pivot
   start is reached by driving with the veto bypassed (the memory updated
   every period, as a person pushing the robot would leave it) to an
   outline gap of 1-15 cm.
6. **The mark (D6 of the design) is not built in B.** `robot/server.py`
   is held by 3.145's claim. It becomes step B2 after 3.145 lands; D6's
   "vetoes at an edge already marked" bar moves with it. B judges D6's
   falls and progress rows.
7. **The seed.** At its first reading the memory takes a 30 cm disc round
   the centre as floor: the robot stands there. That assumes the dock is
   at least 30 cm from any drop; every sweep start honours it.
8. **One memory per robot**, shared by every `SafetyController` over it:
   two memories dead-reckoning apart would place remembered floor apart.
9. **The drop block is on where the house has a drop.** A house without
   one senses exactly what it did (D8's fingerprints); the flat-floor
   sweep turns it on.

### Amendment 2 (2026-11-07, written before any recorded measurement)

Trial missions (none recorded; the sweeps begun beforehand were stopped
and discarded, their code mid-change) showed the rule as built refusing
on floor with no drop: 71-404 `drop_unseen` vetoes a mission in the
walled-off houses. Four changes, each from a dump of the memory at a
false veto:

1. **The blind band is floor when the ring is seen.** The camera never
   sees from the bumper to its MinZ ring; after a turn in a tight place
   that band was never remembered and the robot could not drive on. When
   every corridor column sees floor at the ring in the frame, the band
   (half a cell wider, so the corridor search's own lattice is inside it)
   is marked floor: a straight edge cannot cross the band without crossing
   the ring inside the corridor. An edge already remembered stays.
   Inferred geometry, judged by D1-D3 (the approach sweep's corners and
   openings at up to 70 degrees).
2. **Turns refuse at a remembered edge only**, not at unseen floor: a
   corner swings at most 5.5 cm past the outline, so the worst unseen can
   do is overhang, never fall. D2's turn-sweep bar judges it on truth.
3. **Walls are lines and cast shadows.** Adjacent columns that end on a
   thing within 10 cm are joined; a frame's floor is marked before its
   ends, so no column paints over another's end; an unseen cell whose
   sightline from the lens crosses a remembered thing, or that lies
   outward of a thing on its side of the corridor, is behind it and ends
   that line (the body stops at the wall first: cone, corridor, band).
4. **D6's missions** (the in-process frontier agent never leaves its
   first room, so it measures nothing): the explore policy, find and
   survey jobs, with nav2 stood in for by `VettedNav` -- it plans over the
   true floor (a drop is open to it, as to nav2 without the mark) and
   drives each path through the wheel loop's `vet_wheel_velocity()`, as
   nav2's twists reach the wheels. 20 starts per stairs house per job, the
   same starts walled off; rooms reached counted from the truth; 5,000
   ticks a mission (a run at the cap is recorded as capped).

**Why D2 and D4 failed (inferred from the rows, not yet proven by a
dump):** the pivot check rotates outline points 5 degrees and refuses
when one lands in a remembered `edge` cell. Cells are 5 cm, so within
about one cell of an edge a point can land in the edge cell while its
true gap grows (the 20 away refusals, all at a gap under 6 cm), and one
wheel-loop step of a toward turn starting under 1 cm can cross the lip
before the cell flips (the 0.17 cm overhang). Neither gap is reachable
with the rule on: the approach sweep's smallest stop is 11.42 cm, and a
refused away turn leaves reverse over driven floor, refused 1 time in 300.
The fix (a gap-aware pivot test against the edge's cells) is B2's,
measured on this sweep again.
