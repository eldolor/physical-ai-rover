# 3.213 A false close-range sighting replaces a good approach

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.213; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** In `brain/explore.py` (lines 607-614 on `dev` dd1435a2), any new
sighting more than `APPROACH_MOVED_M` (0.5 m) from the current approach
replaces it. 3.210.B's sizing (read only, the broad sweep on `dev`) found that
in about half of 180 ProcTHOR val finds a good approach (its sighting within
0.5 m of the target) is replaced by a false one (more than 1.0 m off): 389
times in 90 rows under the rooms chooser, 511 in 91 under rule. 5-7% of those
rows end not found. Many false sightings are placed from very short range
(0.16-0.17 m in val:44). This is also the root of 3.210's reverse crowding (E2
and E4 FAILED there). What it costs in time was never measured, and nothing is
built until that is known (the orchestrator, under the user's delegation,
2026-11-22: measure first).

**What a false sighting is, in the simulator (traced on val:44, `dev`
dd1435a2).** The simulator's detector reports true labels only, at the
bearing of the target's visible part (`sim/grid_world.py` `_detections`). So
a false sighting is not a false detection. It is a true detection placed at
the wrong range: explore ranges it with the lidar at the detection's bearing,
and the beams there hit something nearer than the target. In val:44 the
sightings 2.74 m and 1.73 m off were ranged 0.16 m and 0.17 m (a dining table)
while the detector's own distance read 2.70 m and 2.18 m. A 2.27 m range
against a 3.02 m detection hit a wall. None of those lidar windows read as an
edge (3.69 C's two-surface rule), so the rule meant for this case did not fire.

## 3.213.A Measure (records only)

**Harness.** `evaluations/false-sighting-3213/measure.py`: 3.210.B's broad
sweep unchanged (`usable()[30:210]`, 180 houses, seed 0, red backpack, no
zones, `sweep_c1.py`'s `run`), under both choosers, in two arms:

* `dev`: explore as it is. It records every sighting (its error, the lidar
  range it was placed at, the bearing, the detector's distance, whether the
  window was an edge, and what the point lies on in the house as built) and
  every approach. That includes the case `broad.py` missed: a sighting within
  the standoff cancels the live goal without sending one (`kind:
  at_target`).
* `oracle`: the same, except that ground truth withholds every replacement
  of a live approach goal whose sighting is good (within 0.5 m) by a sighting
  that is false (more than 1.0 m off). The tick waits on the live goal, which
  is what explore does when a sighting has not moved. The sighting is still
  kept as seen. No real rule can do better than this, so the oracle's saving
  is the most any rule could buy.

The desktop lock is held shared for every run, `SWEEP_WORKERS=4`.

**Definitions (written before measuring).**
* An *affected row* has at least one good-by-false replacement on `dev`
  (`replaced` true, `replaced_err_m` <= 0.5, `err_m` > 1.0, of either kind).
* *Both-found rows* end `found` in both arms. A row's time to find is its
  `sim_s`.
* A row's *saving* is (`dev` `sim_s` - `oracle` `sim_s`) / `dev` `sim_s`.

**The stop rule (written before measuring).** The section goes on to a
proposed rule only if, under either chooser, at least one of these holds:

| # | Trigger |
|---|---|
| A1 | The median saving over affected both-found rows is **>= 2%** |
| A2 | The p90 of `sim_s` over all both-found rows falls **>= 2%** from `dev` to `oracle` |
| A3 | `oracle` finds **>= 3** more rows than `dev` |

If none holds under either chooser, the time cost is small even at the
oracle's bound, and the section closes with its records. If one holds, the
proposed rule and step B's bars go to the orchestrator, and nothing is built
before its go.

**Characterised (records, no bar).** For the false sightings that replaced a
good approach, against all good sightings, the following are recorded: the
lidar range; the range over the detector's distance; the edge flag; what the
point lies on; the bearing; and whether a later sighting in the same row
agrees (within 0.5 m) with the replaced good place, or with the false one.
The simulator's detector reports no confidence, so confidence cannot be
characterised here. That is recorded as not measurable in the simulator.

* Time: over the broad sweep's both-found rows (branch and `dev`), the median

* Found: **>=** `dev`'s in every arm. That covers 3.192's four find arms

What this says:
* **The title's premise is wrong. Close range is rare.** Only 3% of the false
  sightings that replaced a good approach were ranged under 0.3 m. val:44's
  0.16 m case is real, but it is the exception. A typical false sighting is
  ranged 3 m away, on a wall (about 60%) or a piece of furniture, and lies
  past or short of the target. The title stays as reserved; this record
  governs.
* **What tells them apart in the simulator is the range against the
  detector's own distance.** For a good sighting that ratio is tight (0.64 to
  0.93). For a false one it spreads from 0.36 to 1.9. False sightings also
  sit further off-centre: a median 19-20 deg against 14 deg, near the edge of
  the 30-deg cone.
* **3.69 C's edge flag does not tell them apart** (41-57% against 44-49%).
* **Seeing a sighting again is weak confirmation.** 67-74% of the false
  places are seen again (the same viewpoint mis-ranges the same way), against
  93-95% for the replaced good places.
* Detector confidence is not measurable in the simulator (no field).

## 3.213.B Probes, then the build

**The candidates (written before any probe run).** Each acts only where a new
sighting would replace a **live approach goal** (both kinds: a goal sent, or
the cancel inside the standoff). When it withholds the replacement, the tick
waits on the live goal, as the oracle's does. The sighting is still kept as
seen (`_last_seen`, `_note_seen_at`). Nothing changes when no approach is
live.

* **C1, keep the better-supported.** Every sighting of the mission is
  counted, one a tick. A place's *support* is the number of those within
  `SUPPORT_M` = 0.5 m of it, the new sighting included. The live approach is
  replaced only if the new place's support is **greater than** the live
  approach's place's. The comparison is strict because a good place is seen
  again far more often than a false one (93-95% against 67-74%, step A).
* **C2, the depth edge (3.204.B's rule).** `brain/inventory.py`
  `straddles_edge(scan_returns(scan), bearing, EDGE_GAP_M)` at the sighting's
  bearing, with the gate (`RANGE_GATE_DEG` = 2) and gap (1.0 m) as 3.204.B
  chose them. A sighting whose gate straddles an edge never replaces a live
  approach. It may still start one when none is live, so a first approach
  is never lost to it.
* **C1+C2.** Withheld if either withholds.

**Harness.** `measure.py --arm c1|c2|c1c2`, the broad sweep as in step A,
both choosers, scored against step A's `dev` rows (same commit).

**Selection (written before any probe run).** A probe *qualifies* if, under
both choosers, all of these hold against `dev` on the broad sweep:
* found >= `dev`;
* the median and the p90 `sim_s` over both-found rows are each <= `dev`'s;
* the median saving over affected both-found rows is >= 1%.

The *winner* is the qualifying probe with the largest total `sim_s` saved
over both-found rows, summed over the two choosers. A tie within 1% goes to
the candidate without C2, since C2 needs Rover data. If no probe qualifies,
the section records that and stops. The broad sweep's qualification is
necessary for step B but not sufficient: the full bars still run on the
build.

**Winner: C1.** C1+C2 is within 1% of it (3 s more), so the tie goes to the
candidate without C2, as written. C1 matches the ground-truth oracle, and
beats it on time (the oracle saved 17,355 s). The likely reason is that C1

**Amendment 1 (written 2026-11-22, before its run).** Both reviewers
(`/code-review`, the Thermos pass) flagged the same thing. C1 also withholds
the cancel inside the standoff, so a well-supported live approach can carry
the robot away from a sighting within arm's reach. That cost is delay only:
`_last_seen` keeps the near place, and `_back_to_sighting` takes it once the
live goal ends. The case is measured, not argued. Probe **C1s** is C1 except
that a sighting within `APPROACH_STANDOFF_M` + 0.05 m of the robot is never
withheld. It runs from the `dev` control checkout, under both choosers,
scored as the probes were. C1s is built in place of C1 only if all of these
hold: it qualifies under the selection rule; its found count is >= C1's
under each chooser; and its total `sim_s` saved is >= 99% of C1's. Otherwise
C1 stays and the case is recorded as a known trade-off. The other review
fixes (the refusal prune and the cap) are not in the probes. The step B bars
run on the final code and decide; the probes only choose.

**Amendment 1 measured.** Rows are `B-probes/{rooms,rule}_c1s.json`. C1s
finds 180 and 180, the same as C1, and qualifies. Its median `sim_s` is 89.95

Withholding the cancel inside the standoff is a known trade-off: it costs
delay only, and in the simulator it saves more time than it costs.
