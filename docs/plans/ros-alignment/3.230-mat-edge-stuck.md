# 3.230 The Rover stuck at a mat's edge: traction, not an obstacle

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.230; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user, 2026-11-27 evening, during 3.229.B's survey (with a
photo): the Rover sat "stuck on a mat for a while". It was at the edge of a
doormat, the mat's lip under its front left wheel, beside a door's hinge
corner. The brain's log at the time (t ~ 450-476 s):

* `FORWARD (blocked) -- stopped after 0.2cm through ROS -- the wheels did
  not carry it out (the safety vet, or the chain, held them)`;
* `REVERSE (blocked) -- 0.0cm < min=3cm (astern_occluded)` (the blind
  rear, 3.218);
* the turns ran (`turned_deg` 96);
* `small_room goal stalled (0.02 m from its start); backing out`.

So the lidar saw nothing ahead: the mat's lip is a centimetre or two high,
below the lidar's plane. The wheels were asked to drive and did not carry
the robot over it. Nothing in the stack knows about a **low step that holds
the wheels**:
* the safety layer judges obstacles, not traction;
* 3.35's stall detection stops the motors and calls it a fault;
* explore's escape tries REVERSE (refused: the blind rear), a 0.5 s creep
  at verb speed 50 (too little to climb), then turns on the spot. A
  skid-steer turn with one wheel on the lip scrubs and does not lift it.

The robot leaves only by luck, or by a turn that happens to bring it off
the lip.

**What is not known yet.** The log line does not say which held the wheels:
(a) the robot server's vet; (b) 3.35's stall detection, wheels commanded
but no wheel feedback; or (c) wheels turning with no progress (slip on the
mat's pile, wheel odometry moving while the SLAM pose holds). Each needs a
different fix. Step A settles it from the run's own trace (3.229.B's
`~/survey3229.jsonl` and the stack's INFO log, on the board) before
anything is built.

**The idea (to be confirmed by A, then given bars).**

1. **Tell traction from an obstacle.** A forward was commanded with open
   clearance ahead (lidar and the floor band clear past the bar), and the
   body did not move. The two measures are wheel feedback against the
   command, and the SLAM pose against the wheel odometry. That is
   *traction-stuck*: wheels held is a step, wheels spinning is slip. It is
   not an obstacle, and not a motor-board fault.
2. **Climb, bounded.** A traction-stuck robot facing open space gets one
   short straight push at a higher, capped speed and duration (a "climb"),
   square to the edge. It never turns on the lip. The push goes through
   `SafetyController` like every move (rule 4): the forward clearance must
   be open past the bar at the climb's speed. The server's watchdog and
   stall detection still apply, and 3.35's limits bound how long the
   motors may push against a hold.
3. **Then give up on the place.** If the climb fails, mark the spot as a
   keepout, a low step the lidar cannot see, using the robot server's
   existing wedge-watch keepout discs (3.228 cause 3b). nav2 then routes
   around it, and the mark stays with the map (a saved map's no-go spots)
   so the next mission avoids the mat's edge.
4. **See it coming (later, with 3.222).** The OAK-D floor band can see a
   step at the floor. A step under a set height (decided in its own step)
   can then be crossed square and at climb speed; a higher one becomes a
   keepout before the robot touches it.

**Criteria.** Written in each step before it is measured (CLAUDE.md
section 7). A's are below. B-E's are written at the end of A, from what A
finds.

* **A1.** For the 3.229.B run's stuck window(s) at the mat, report per
  forward attempt: the command, the robot server's stop reason, whether
  3.35's stall detection fired, the wheel feedback (pulses), and the SLAM
  pose's change. Name the cause as (a), (b) or (c) only if the trace shows
  it; otherwise name the record that would settle it.
* **A2.** The same count over every stall of the run, so the mat's case is
  set against the others. Gives a rate: how many of the run's stalls were
  traction, not geometry.
