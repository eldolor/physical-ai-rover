# 3.197 A boxed robot frees only its own neighbourhood, not the whole zone

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.197; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** (the orchestrator, on the user's delegation, 2026-11-21) 3.193 made
the bridge's keepout mask mark every cell a zone touches, and added D4's
boxed rule: every zone that draws one of the robot's five cells (its own and
four neighbours) is left out WHOLE when the drawn zones together draw all
five, until the robot is 0.30 m clear. 3.195 grew each zone by 0.05 m with
rounded corners, and takes off only the margin when the margin boxes the
robot. At slanted edges and at corners a robot can still be boxed and switch
a whole zone off (3.195's "Left": 641 of 12,000 positions without the
margin, 558 with it). On the Rover the zones are rotated by its start
heading, so every zone edge is slanted against the costmap's lattice.

## 3.197.A: criteria and design (written 2026-11-21, before building)

**Baseline measured on `dev` first** (`evaluations/nogo-3197/sweep.py` on
`dev`'s `keepout`, `sweep-dev.json`; the bridge's whole step, margins then
D4, as `Bridge._zones_step` runs it):
* **3.195's 558 was `suspend()` alone.** On 3.195's own sampling (robots
  0-40 mm outside the drawn edge of rotated zones, away from corners; S1b
  below) `dev`'s whole step leaves **0** of 12,000 zones out: 3.195's D2
  takes the margin off first. 3.195's number was measured before D2.
* **Where `dev` still switches a zone off (S1):** one zone at each of six
  headings (0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 75 degrees, +-2) on four lattice phases, 500
  robots each from 0.05 m inside the zone to 0.04 m outside its drawn edge,
  around the whole boundary: **2,913 of 12,000** zones left out whole for
  boxing (341 at 0 degrees, 651 at 45). 158 of them with the robot outside
  the zone (corners); 2,755 with it 0-0.05 m inside, where the bare zone's
  slanted touched cells box it.
* **And where `dev` cannot plan out at all:** two zones 0-0.12 m apart
  (S5, 9,000 robots): **1,122** positions where no 4-connected walk through
  undrawn cells leaves the robot's cell (a slanted gap's touched cells leave
  only a diagonal line of free cells, which a 4-connected planner cannot
  use; the robot has a free 4-neighbour, so 3.193's E6 promise holds and
  D4 does nothing). Walks with the state carried (S6, 10,000 steps): 122
  such steps, 16 whole-zone suspensions.

**D1. A vent, not a suspension.** When the zones as drawn (after 3.195's
margins and D4's depth and fresh rules) leave the robot no way out -- no
4-connected walk through undrawn cells from its own cell (which NavFn frees)
to a cell `VENT_REACH_CELLS` (10, 0.50 m) away -- the bridge undraws the
fewest cells that open one: a cheapest path from the robot's cell to that
radius, each drawn cell on it costing 1 if it is drawn only by a margin, 4
if it touches a zone as drawn by the person, 16 if its centre is inside one
(so a vent goes through margins and slivers before a zone's body). The vent
is at most `VENT_MAX_CELLS` (10; 20 since amendment 1) cells, on the mask's lattice; its cells
are written unknown in `keepout.mask()` (`holes`) after the zones and
before the spots (a wedge spot is never vented). Logged `zone_vent <n>
cells` / `zone_vent_moved` / `zone_vent_closed` (amendment 2); `/health`'s `nogo` gains `vent_cells` and
`vents` (a counter).
* **Kept** while the robot can plan out with it; **replaced** (from the
  robot's cell, as above) when it cannot; **closed** once the robot could
  plan out with no vent at its pose and at the four points `HYST_M` (0.01
  m) from it (3.195's hysteresis, so a mm of jitter does not flap it).
* **Cleared** on a change of lattice phase (the bridge re-runs the step on
  the new lattice) and when the zone set is replaced.
* **No vent within `VENT_MAX_CELLS`:** the old rule, unchanged -- every
  zone drawing one of the robot's five cells is left out whole (D4's
  suspension, restored at 0.30 m).

**D2. What does not change.** `keepout.suspend()`'s own behaviour (the
boxed rule stays its default; the bridge calls it with `boxed=False` and
vents instead), `margins_off()` (3.195 D2), the depth (0.05 m) and fresh
rules, LEAVE_M, the spots' drawing, the routes (none new; the wall linter's
cap unchanged), `nav2.yaml`, `brain/`, `control/`, `world/`, `robot/`. The
one change outside `service/slam/` is a test hook: **`SIM_START_TURN_DEG`**
(`sim/maps/__init__.py build_world`) turns the simulated robot's start
heading, unset by default, so the live runs can put the zones at a slant to
SLAM's lattice as the Rover's start heading will.

## 3.197.A amendment 1 (written 2026-11-21, after the first build's sweep, before any live run)

The first build (`VENT_MAX_CELLS` = 10) met V1-V3 and V5-V7 in process but
**failed V4: 998 whole-zone suspensions in S5** (bar 510;
`sweep-build1.json`). Every one of the 998 is a robot in a slanted gap of
0-0.12 m between two zones, where the cheapest way out runs along the gap
to its end: 11-14 cells (a 4-connected staircase along a 30-60 degree lane
is about 1.4 times its length in cells). Leaving both zones out whole frees
far more than such a vent, so the cap is raised to **20** cells (the longest
staircase from the window's centre to its edge). V4 is recorded failed for
build 1 and judged as written on build 2. V6's bar (S1's vents: 10 cells,
0.60 m) is unchanged.

## 3.197.A amendment 2 (written 2026-11-21, after both reviews, before any counted live run)

Both reviews (`/code-review` and the Thermos pass, Sonnet) found no
important bug; their nits change D1 as follows:
* **A kept vent is bounded.** A vent with more than `VENT_MAX_CELLS` cells,
  or a cell farther than `VENT_FAR_M` (0.60 m, V6's bound) from the robot,
  is re-derived from the robot's cell (a robot sliding along an edge left an
  old vent behind; a phase move could grow one).
* **A phase move carries the vent** onto the new lattice (every new cell an
  old one overlaps) instead of dropping it, so the mask published on a move
  with no fresh pose keeps the way out; the next pose bounds it again.
* **A re-post of the same zones keeps the vent** (and does not count a new
  one); a changed set is vented afresh.
* **The fallback** (no vent of `VENT_MAX_CELLS`) leaves out whole the zones
  the cheapest way out runs through, not "every zone drawing one of the five
  cells" (which could name a zone that blocks nothing, or none). It is
  broader than 3.193's all-five test on purpose: the robot can have a free
  4-neighbour and still no way out.
* The log is `zone_vent <n> cells` (no ids); `/health`'s `vents` counts
  openings, not moves (`zone_vent_moved` lines).

**What the live runs did not show.** No vent opened in any live run: the
controller never brought the robot close enough to a zone to be boxed, on
either heading (the `b` runs on `dev` had no suspension either). The vent's
behaviour is therefore measured in process only (V1-V7); the live runs show
that it costs nothing where it is not needed (L1-L5, and the same
find times as 3.195).

**Reviews** (all on Sonnet): `/code-review` (high) and the Thermos pass on
the build, then a `/code-review` of the fix commit. No important bug.
Fixed: a re-post reset the vent and counted it again; a phase move with no
pose published a mask with no vent; the fallback could name a zone that
blocked nothing, or none; a carried vent was unbounded (now 20 cells and
0.60 m from the robot); a phase move could grow a vent fourfold; the test
imported the wrong `sweep.py` under xdist; the turn hook's error; the eval
scripts' missing-data cases. Left: the vent ignores walls (3.193's boxed rule
did too); the vent is a one-cell corridor for NavFn, and keepout is on the
global costmap only, so the controller does not see it.

**After the live runs** (a third review, of the fix commit; Sonnet, no
important finding): a stale vent (too large or too far) skipped the
hysteresis close test, so jitter could drop and re-open it. It now closes
only by that test and is otherwise re-derived. The sweeps are unchanged
(`sweep-3197.json` re-run, identical). The live image predates this; the
change runs only while a vent is open, and no counted run opened one, so
the counted runs took the same code path as the final build.

**Left.**
* A live case that opens a vent (a robot placed boxed at a slanted edge, or
  a slanted two-zone gap) -- the in-process sweeps carry the evidence now.
* The vent ignores walls and the SLAM map (as 3.193's rule did).
