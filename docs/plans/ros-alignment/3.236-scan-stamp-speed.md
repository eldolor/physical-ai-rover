# 3.236 Speed on the car

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.236; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** nav2 drives at 0.20 m/s (`desired_linear_vel`), the FORWARD verb
at 0.3 m/s, and the chassis cap is 0.6 m/s. The user said yes (2026-11-27)
to raising the speed on the car, in the order decided 2026-11-07 (CLAUDE.md
3b, "3.42 / 3.76 A4"): **first** the scan's time stamp, **then** braking
measured on the car, **then** speed set by clearance
(`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` 6.9 (a), (c), (d)). The same error in time is a
bigger error in metres at speed (`HANDOFF-2026-10-07-lidar-and-speed.md`
item 5), and 3.44's speed-dependent stop (20 cm up to ~0.34 m/s, 25 cm at
0.4, 35.5 at 0.5) rests on `DECEL_M_S2` = 0.5, a `[PLACEHOLDER]`.

**What is already true (read 2026-11-28, before writing the criteria).**
The stamp fix the 2026-11-07 decision asked for was **built in 3.221.A**
(on `dev` since `7c2b270b`, 2026-11-26), because mapping while turning
needed it first:

* `robot/lidar_ld19.py` fixes each revolution's wall-clock midpoint once,
  when it completes (`capture_unix`); the robot server's `/scan` stamps
  `stamp_unix` with it and says so (`stamp_source: capture`). A body with
  no capture clock (the simulator's instant scan) keeps the request time,
  which is honest for a scan cast at the request.
* The bridge (`picar_bridge` `_poll_scan()`) stamps the ROS `LaserScan`
  with `stamp_unix` while the two clocks agree within 0.5 s, and sends no
  revolution twice.
* 3.221 pinned this with an injected clock (its 6a). Its real-timing bars
  (6b, 6c) are on the Rover and unrun (3.221.B, the user's go).

So step A here builds no new stamp. It **measures** the shipped one against
ground truth under the fake lidar's real timing, which 3.221 did not, pins
that in the suite, and writes the live ROS check for a desktop half (this
cloud machine has no Docker daemon). If A's bars fail, A fixes the stamp.

**Criteria for A (written 2026-11-28, before measuring; CLAUDE.md section 7).**
Ground truth is the emitter's own clock: `sim/fake_lidar.py`'s `Emitter`
casts point k at `t0 + k / 4500` s, so revolution r's true midpoint is
known exactly. The scans travel a real pty to the real driver
(`Ld19Lidar`, its reader thread) and out through the robot server's `/scan`
route, polled at the bridge's 10 Hz with a random phase.

1. **The stamp is the capture time, on ground truth.** Over a 60 s run
   (at least 550 distinct revolutions), `stamp_unix` minus the true
   midpoint of that revolution: **|median| <= 5 ms, p95 of |error| <= 10 ms,
   max <= 25 ms.** Every reply `stamp_source: capture`.
   Contrast (the progress half): the same replies' request time minus the
   true midpoint, i.e. the error before 3.221; reported, expected 50-200 ms.
2. **The age nav2 is handed is honest.** Request time minus `stamp_unix`
   in **[0, 250 ms]** for every reply, and the median gap between
   successive distinct stamps **90-110 ms** (3.221 6b's bars, here in the
   simulator under real timing).
3. **The same under load.** Criterion 1's run repeated with every core but
   one busy (a CPU burner per core): p95 of |error| **<= 15 ms**, max
   **<= 50 ms**, 0 replies outside criterion 2's window. Reported beside 1.
4. **Pinned.** A suite test runs a short version (3 s) and fails if the
   median |error| exceeds 10 ms, any |error| exceeds 50 ms, or the
   request-stamp contrast is not at least 3x the capture error's median
   (so a regression back to request stamps fails it). It is run in its
   module's real order and under the fast tier's xdist.
5. **Nothing else changes.** No change to `robot/safety.py` or the robot
   server's veto (the diff); `tests/test_wall_linters.py`,
   `tests/test_ros_containment.py`, `tests/test_rover_slam.py`,
   `tests/test_lidar_safety.py` and the fast tier pass.
6. **Desktop half (the live check, not run here):** on the desktop's live
   stack (`SIM_LIDAR=fake`, `ROBOT_MODE=hardware`, `SIM_MOTOR_BOARD=fake`,
   `ROBOT_DRIVE=ros`, `WORLD_MODE=ros`, `SIM_MAP=scaled_house`), 60 s of
   `/scan` as ROS receives it: ROS time minus `header.stamp` in
   [0, 250 ms] for every message, median gap 90-110 ms, `scans_repeated`
   counted and `scans_published` at most the revolutions completed; no
   "outdated" warning from `collision_monitor` and no costmap
   message-filter drop in the container log; then the nav suite's
   smoothness bar (reversals per metre <= 1.0, 3.42 criterion 5, failed 2
   of 3 on 2026-10-07 with request stamps) on 3 runs. Steps below.

**Criteria for B and C** are written in their own parts below, before
either is run.

## B: braking on the Rover (written, not run)

**Why.** `DECEL_M_S2` = 0.5 m/s^2 is a guess; 3.44's bands and any faster
nav2 rest on it (6.9 (d): measured stopping at each band before the sim's
bands are trusted).

**Criteria (written 2026-11-28, before measuring).** Ground truth is a tape
on the floor and the robot server's own wheel odometry at 0.1 mm (the
fork's odometers), cross-checked by the tape.

1. For each band **0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 m/s**, 5 runs on the house's
   hard floor and 5 on its rug: the distance from the stop command's send
   to rest. The fitted deceleration per band is the 10th percentile of the
   runs (the worst stopper sets it).
2. `stop_distance_cm(v)` with the measured deceleration covers every
   measured stop at every band (0 runs past the formula); if not, B
   records it as failed and C does not start.
3. Progress: the new `DECEL_M_S2` and the band where 20 cm stops holding,
   against 3.44's ~0.34 m/s.

**Run steps (the user beside the Rover, the board lock held, wheels on the
floor, a clear 3 m straight):**

1. `python tools/jetson/board_lock.py hold --why "3.236.B braking"`; stop
   `picar-stack`'s brain (the robot server stays: every move goes through
   `SafetyController`).
2. Tape a start line and a 3 m lane; a phone filming the side at 60 fps
   for the tape cross-check.
3. Per run: `POST /wheels` at the band's speed under driver `twin-dpad`
   until odometry reads 1.0 m past the start, then `POST /action` STOP;
   log the stop's send time and the wheel odometry at 20 Hz until two
   samples agree within 0.5 mm (rest).
4. A small script (`evaluations/braking-3236/measure.py`, written with B)
   reads the log: distance from send to rest, per run and band.
5. Put the board back as found; release the lock; record the numbers here.

2. (a) On `scaled_house` and `home_first_floor`, 3.15's goal success and
