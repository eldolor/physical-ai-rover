# 3.145 A nav2 goal outlives a dead or hung brain (3.135.B F6g, F7g)

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.145; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** 3.135.B's rows F6g and F7g FAILED on the Jetson, 5 of 5 each
(2026-11-04, the harness at `3.135.B`'s branch on `dev` `a23476f4`+, 15 W,
sim mode, `drive: ros`, judged on the simulator's ground truth). 3.135
criterion 3 makes a failed row its own section, and this is theirs: one
cause. The fix and its criteria are this section's to write; the finding is
below.

* *The runs:* a plain `explore` mission in `scaled_house` with the ROS
  container (SLAM + nav2). The fault fires 0.15 s into a FORWARD while the
  robot server holds an active nav2 goal and no brain verb holds the robot
  (amendments 5, 10, 11).
  * F6g: SIGKILL the brain.
  * F7g: SIGSTOP the brain 30 s, then SIGCONT.
* *The numbers* (bars: wheels zero <= 1.1 s, true path over the next 6 s
  <= 15 cm):
  * F6g: wheels zero **2.80-3.33 s** after the kill, **16.9-18.5 cm** moved.
  * F7g: **2.79-3.15 s**, **17.1-18.3 cm**.
  * F7g's other half met: no wheel setpoint in the 10 s after SIGCONT.
  * 0 contacts and 0 late ticks in every run; a new mission driving 4.9-6.6
    s later; the board left as found.
* *The cause:* under `drive: ros`, nav2's wheel commands take the
  actuator's route (`/wheels`, driver `ros`), which is not arbitrated and
  keeps the robot server's watchdog fed. A goal the brain sent therefore
  drives on to its end (~3 s, ~18 cm here) after the brain that owns it has
  died or frozen. The B3.1 watchdog guards a silent driver, and nav2 is not
  silent. On the desktop the planted watchdog changed nothing (3.135.B's
  criterion 4 record, `desktop/b-goal/`).
* *A design question, put to the user by the orchestrating session
  (2026-11-04):* should a brain-owned nav2 goal outlive its brain under
  `drive: ros`? The answer decides the fix; the rows are scored as written.
* *Records:* `evaluations/jetson-fmea-3135/board/rows/F6g-20261104-0825/`, `evaluations/jetson-fmea-3135/board/rows/F7g-20261104-0828/` (on
  `plan/3.135.B-single-faults` until 3.135.B merges), BOARD-BENCH's fault
  table.
* *Re-test:* `python evaluations/jetson-fmea-3135/run.py row F6g --runs 5`
  and `row F7g --runs 5` (board); `plant_bugs.py --rows F6g,F7g` (desktop).
  The safety path and who-drives arbitration make this risky (CLAUDE.md
  section 6).

**The design question, decided (2026-11-06, the orchestrating session on
the user's standing instruction to take the recommended option):** no. A
goal the brain sent is the brain driving; it lives only while the brain
does. Goals naming a person or no one are unchanged.

**The fix (to build).** A lease on the brain's goal, on the watchdog's own
clock (`watchdog_timeout_s`, 1.0 s):

* The brain's navigator beats `POST /autonomy/heartbeat` (its `x-driver`)
  every 0.25 s on its own thread, from its creation to its close, so a long
  tick (a VLM call) never starves it; only a dead or frozen process does.
* The robot server arms a lease when it accepts a goal from an autonomous
  driver (`brain`) that has beaten within the lease. A brain that never
  beats (one older than this section) keeps today's behaviour.
* No beat for `watchdog_timeout_s`: the watchdog loop ends the goal as a
  Stop does (3.84's hold zeroes nav2's wheel posts at once, the goal-ending
  loop cancels it in nav2) and latches autonomy out as `autonomy_lapsed`
  until a mission starts (`POST /autonomy/resume`), so a brain thawed after
  a freeze cannot drive on. A person's Stop latch is never overwritten.
* The brain reads an `autonomy_lapsed` refusal as the end of its mission:
  `failed`, naming it.

**Criteria (written before building; CLAUDE.md section 7):**

1. *In process (the suite, red first):* a goal from a beating brain whose
   beats stop is ended within `watchdog_timeout_s` + 0.1 s of its last
   beat -- nav2's next wheel post held at zero, the goal cancelled -- and
   autonomy is refused `autonomy_lapsed` until `/autonomy/resume`; a goal
   from a brain that beats every 0.25 s survives 5 s with no other command
   (no false lapse); a never-beating brain's goal, a person's goal and an
   unnamed goal are unchanged; a person's Stop latch is kept over a lapse;
   the brain's mission ends `failed` naming `autonomy_lapsed` (a verb's
   refusal and a goal's).
2. *Desktop live stack, as shipped* (`fmea.run_row`, `scaled_house`, ROS
   container, `drive: ros`; 3.135's bars unchanged): F6g and F7g 3 runs
   each, every valid run met -- wheels zero <= 1.1 s, <= 15 cm over the
   next 6 s, 0 contacts; F7g no driving sample in the 10 s after SIGCONT;
   a new mission driving afterwards.
3. *Desktop planted check* (`plant_bugs.py --rows F6g,F7g`, the plant the
   lease switched off): both rows caught AND discriminating (3.135
   criterion 4; before this section they were caught but not
   discriminating).
4. *Progress (section 7.4), desktop live:* 3 plain `explore` missions in
   `scaled_house` end `found`, 0 contacts, 0 lapses (`/health`'s lapse
   count) -- the lease never ends a living brain's goal.
5. *No regression:* the fast tier passes, explore's in-process sweeps and
   pins unchanged (they run with no robot server).
6. *Board (owed until the user is back, after 11-23):* F6g and F7g 5 runs
   each on the Jetson as 3.135.B ran them.
