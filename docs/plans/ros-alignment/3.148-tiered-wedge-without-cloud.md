# 3.148 A tiered mission with no cloud wedges at a jamb and ends blocked (3.135.B F11, F12)

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.148; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** 3.135.B's rows F11a, F11b and F12a FAILED on the Jetson
(2026-11-04, 15 W, sim mode, ground truth). In 6 of their 15 runs the
tiered mission ended `blocked`, which is not an end any of their bars
allows. 3.135 criterion 3 makes it its own section. The fix and its criteria
are this section's to write.

* *The runs:* 3.135 amendment 5's cloud stack. That is a tiered mission for
  the red backpack in `scaled_house`, with no ROS and no local tier
  (`LOCAL_VLM_URL` empty), and `SIM_ARRIVAL_CONFIRM=cloud`. Each row has a
  different cloud:
  * F11a: refused from the start;
  * F11b: the stand-in answers one `/navigate`, then is killed;
  * F12a: the cloud hangs (`hang_cloud.py`).
* *The numbers:*
  * F11a: 1 of 5 runs ended `blocked`;
  * F11b: 3 of 5;
  * F12a: 2 of 5.
  * The other 9 runs ended `arrived_unconfirmed` at 95-111 s, as the bars
    want, after ~460 cm of path.
  * Every `blocked` run ended with "FORWARD refused 5 times in a row by the
    safety layer, 3 times over". That happened at one of two places:
    * 4 runs at (2.97, 1.72) after ~227 cm, at 178-184 s;
    * 2 runs at (4.48, 1.66) after ~376 cm, at 152-154 s.
  * In both places the robot faced east (heading -0.05 to +0.03 rad) at a
    true gap of 19.9-21.9 cm. The refusals were real `safety_distance`
    vetoes off the scan footprint, not 3.144's stale bundle.
* *What held, in every run:*
  * never two paid calls open at once;
  * F12a's arrival parked in 3.8-15.6 s (bar 30 s);
  * F11b's late confirmation reached a final state;
  * 0 contacts, 0 late ticks, 0 OOM, nothing driven after the end;
  * the board was left as found.
  * F11c (`vision`, refused) was met 5/5.
* *Cause, not yet shown:*
  * With no tier able to steer (the cloud down, no local VLM, no nav2),
    the tiered policy drives FORWARD into the same two spots and the stuck
    breaker ends the mission. `brain/tiered.py`'s `STEER_BAND_DEG` note
    records an earlier form of this: drift into a door jamb.
  * The desktop's single shipped run of each row passed (3.135
    amendment 5's planted checks), so this is a rate, not a certainty.
  * No fault-free control was run: whether a tiered mission with the cloud
    up wedges at the same spots is this section's first measurement. If it
    does, the fault is not the cause, and the rows' mover is the problem,
    not the cloud's guards.
* *Records:* `evaluations/jetson-fmea-3135/board/rows/F11a-20261104-1029/`,
  `F11b-20261104-1040/`, `F12a-20261104-1056/` (on
  `plan/3.135.B-single-faults` until 3.135.B merges), and BOARD-BENCH's
  fault table.
* *Re-test:* `run.py row F11a|F11b|F12a --runs 5` (board). Risky by
  CLAUDE.md section 6 if the fix touches retries, timeouts or paid calls.

**Criteria (written before any measurement; CLAUDE.md section 7).** The
board is off-limits until the user is back (the orchestrating session,
2026-11-05), so every run here is on the desktop. The desktop uses
3.135.B's own stack (`cloud_rows.py`'s: `stack.py`, ground truth, no ROS,
no local tier, `SIM_ARRIVAL_CONFIRM=cloud`) on this section's ports, under
the desktop lock (exclusive: the numbers depend on timing). Driver:
`evaluations/tiered-wedge-3148/`.

*Step A, the diagnosis (on `dev` before any fix):*

1. **A1, the fault reproduced:** F11a 10 times. Recorded: how many end
   `blocked`, where (true pose at the end), and after how long.
2. **A2, the control, no fault:** the same stack and mission 10 times with
   a cloud that answers. That cloud is `truth_cloud.py`, $0: from the
   simulator's truth it says "visible, FORWARD" when the target is truly
   ahead and near, else "not visible, RIGHT". Recorded the same way.
3. **The decision, by rule:**
   * if A2 ends `blocked` in at least 1 run at a place within 0.3 m of
     one of A1's (or the board's two places), the wedge is the policy's
     approach, not the missing cloud: the fix is in the approach, and
     criterion 5 applies to A2 too;
   * if A2 has 0 `blocked` and A1 at least 1, the missing cloud is the
     cause: the fix is in what the tiered policy does without a steering
     tier;
   * if A1 has 0 `blocked` in 10, the desktop does not reproduce the
     board, and an amendment is written before anything else.

*Step B, the fix (built red first; measured after the fix):*

4. **An in-process test reproduces the mechanism**: the same `blocked` for
   the cause step A finds. It fails on `dev` and passes with the fix.
5. **The rows hold, on the desktop:** F11a, F11b and F12a 10 times each.
   * 0 `blocked`.
   * Every run ends as its row's reading wants: F11a `arrived_unconfirmed`;
     F11b `arrived_unconfirmed`, or `found` only by a cloud yes before the
     switch; F12a `arrived_unconfirmed` with its park <= 30 s.
   * 0 contacts, never two paid calls open at once, nothing driven after
     the end.
6. **No regression with the cloud up:** A2's control 10 times again, with
   0 `blocked`, at least as many `found` as A2 on `dev`, and 0 contacts.
   The suite's pinned tiered and explore sweeps pass unchanged.
7. **Paid calls:** per run, no more than on `dev` for the same row
   (median over the 10), and never two at once.
8. **Owed, not run here:** the board's re-run (`run.py row F11a|F11b|F12a
   --runs 5`) waits for the user's return. It is recorded as owed, not as
   met.

**The mechanism (reproduced in process).** With no tier able to steer (no
cloud, no local VLM, no nav2), the tiered policy aims straight at the
backpack. From any start line at y >= 1.68 m that line clips the lower jamb
of `scaled_house`'s doors (they span y 0.9-1.8 m). The stuck breaker's
back-off reverses once, and the policy re-aims along the same line; after 3
episodes the mission ends `blocked`. A cloud that answers breaks the line
with its turns, by accident, not design.

**This is a pinned design decision, not a defect.**
* `tests/test_bearing_turns.py` pins it:
  `test_a_door_jamb_on_the_straight_line_is_a_planning_problem_not_a_steering_one`
  and `test_a_robot_pushing_into_a_door_jamb_ends_blocked_not_at_max_steps`.
* So does `control/mission_runner.py`'s `BLOCKED` comment. Going around is
  route planning, nav2's job (R1, 3.4, R6).

**Option (a), built and not merged:** a sidestep in the back-off. After its
REVERSE it turns 30 deg away from the nearest scan return within 45 deg and
1.0 m, then creeps one vetted FORWARD. In process every start line then
arrives, red on `dev` and green with it. It reverses that decision: it
changes the two jamb tests and `test_stuck_retry`'s person in a doorway, and
touches the motion path. It is kept as
`evaluations/tiered-wedge-3148/sidestep-option-a.patch`, with its test
`sidestep_option_a_checks.py` (named so pytest never collects it; run it by name with the patch applied), as the starting point if the Rover ever
shows this wedge with nav2 up. A nav2 that dies mid-mission is 3.146's case,
and `blocked` is the honest end there too.

**Decided (the orchestrating session, under the user's standing instruction,
2026-11-05): option (b), no code change.** Routing stays nav2's job, and the
pinned jamb tests stay as they are.

**Criteria, as written:**
* 1-3 (step A): met, measured; the decision rule applied as written.
* 4: not built into the suite. The test exists only with option (a)'s
  patch.
* 5: **FAILED as written.** `blocked` is not made 0: A1 had 2 of 10 on
  `dev`, and nothing changes it.
* 6-7: not run (no fix to compare).
* 8: the board's re-run is owed only if the rows' reading below is
  checked there; nothing changed to re-test.

**Amendment 1 (2026-11-05, the orchestrating session under the user's
standing instruction): how 3.135.B's F11a, F11b and F12a are read from now
on.** These rows run a tiered mission without ROS: no nav2, no local tier.
In that configuration, `blocked` at a door jamb on the straight line to the
target is the designed end, with "route planning" as its reason (the
`BLOCKED` end; R1, 3.4, R6). It counts as a reading the rows allow, beside
`arrived_unconfirmed`, not as a failure. On the Rover the tiered search runs
with nav2: `tier_search: explore` needs it, and nav2 routes around a jamb.
So the wedge needs a configuration the car does not run.
