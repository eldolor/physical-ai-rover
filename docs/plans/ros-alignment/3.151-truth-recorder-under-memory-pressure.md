# 3.151 The simulator's truth recorder holds under memory pressure (3.135.B F2)

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.151; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** 3.135.B's row F2 (memory runs out during a mission) FAILED on the
Jetson, but only on its instrument (2026-11-04, 15 W, sim mode, at
`1330ea8f`). The user's choice (2026-11-04): harden the recorder in this
section, keep F2 FAILED as written, and re-run F2 as a new record after this
merges. The criteria are this section's to write.

* *The run:* the full VE stack (ROS, VE resident, the stand-in cloud, the
  stack at `oom_score_adj` -900). `tools/jetson/memory_hog.py` runs to the
  first OOM kill during a nav2 goal.
* *What held (every run that started, 4 of 4):*
  * the OOM killer took VE's driver only;
  * the robot server, the brain and ROS stayed alive;
  * 0 late wheel ticks, and every mission ended with an outcome;
  * the planted run (VE at OOM score 0) was caught, because the kernel
    killed the hog instead.
* *What failed:* the simulator's ground-truth log
  (`evaluations/jetson-fmea-3135/truth_body.py`, the body process that
  records pose, the fake board's setpoints and the true gap at 50 Hz) had
  holes of **0.69 s, 1.04 s and 3.01 s** at **15-99 MB** `MemAvailable`
  (bar 0.2 s; 3.135 amendment 2: a hole in any window a bar reads fails the
  run, and contacts read the whole log).
* *Cause, as far as the records show:* the body process already runs at
  -900, so it is not killed. It stalls under memory pressure instead, on
  page faults and direct reclaim of its pages. The robot server's wheel
  loop did not run late, so the stall is the instrument's, and a real
  Rover's physics would not pause.
* *Direction (the user's words: "memory-lock the truth body"):* for
  example, `mlockall()` in the body process, with its memory measured
  first and the board's headroom checked. How, and the bars, are this
  section's call. A planted check (the lock off) should show the hole come
  back.
* *Records:* `evaluations/jetson-fmea-3135/board/rows/F2-20261104-1504/`
  and `F2-planted-ve_oom0-20261104-1456/` (on `plan/3.135.B-single-faults`
  until 3.135.B merges), and BOARD-BENCH's fault table.
* *Re-test:* `run.py row F2 --runs 5` (board, under the board lock), as a
  new record beside 3.135.B's FAILED one. Not on the robot's own path
  (harness only), but it writes recorded data: `/code-review`, plus the
  Thermos pass if it touches the recorder's writes.

**Measured before the criteria (2026-11-05, desktop):** the body process
(`truth_body.py`, idle) holds 72 MB resident (46 MB anonymous, 26 MB file)
but maps 1,133 MB (19 threads' stacks, numpy's and uvicorn's reservations).
The desktop's memlock limit is 8 MB (hard), with no sudo: no lock can be
shown here. The board's is 975,328 kB (~952 MB, soft and hard) and the
board's sudo is the development grant (memory `jetson-dev-sudo`). A plain
`mlockall(MCL_CURRENT)` would be refused on the board (its check is the
whole mapped size against the limit) and, if allowed, would populate the
stacks and reservations -- hundreds of MB taken from a board whose floor is
500 MB. Hence `MCL_ONFAULT`: lock only what is touched.

**Criteria (written before building; CLAUDE.md section 7):**

1. **The lock.** `truth_body.py` gains `POST /fmea/mlock`, which calls
   `mlockall(MCL_CURRENT | MCL_FUTURE | MCL_ONFAULT)` and answers what
   happened (`locked`, the errno and its name, the soft and hard memlock
   limits, `VmLck` and `VmRSS` after); a refusal is reported, never hidden
   or retried silently. `stack.py` locks the body right after it is healthy,
   by default: on a machine with `sudo -n` it first raises the body's
   memlock limit (`prlimit --pid <body> --memlock=unlimited:unlimited`),
   then posts the lock, and writes the answer to the run's `body_lock.json`
   (`FMEA_TRUTH_LOCK=0` skips both: the planted check). The run record says
   whether the truth was locked.
2. **Sampling never waits on the file.** The recorder takes each sample on
   its own thread (the board's lock, the geometry, as before) and hands the
   line to a writer thread; a write that blocks does not delay the next
   sample. The rows are the same fields, values and order as before (a
   test against the old loop's output on one scripted run), and the writer
   drains whatever it holds when the process exits (SIGTERM through
   uvicorn's shutdown). Pinned red first: a writer made to block 1 s leaves
   no gap over 0.2 s between samples (the old loop: a 1 s gap).
3. **The memory cost, on the board** (no motion, the board lock, the stack
   and the local VLM held off as every bench does, left as found): the
   body's `VmRSS` and `VmLck` idle and after 60 s of recording, locked and
   unlocked. Bar: locking adds at most 64 MB resident.
4. **The holes, on the board, no motion:** the truth body alone (sim, its
   recorder running, the fake board idle), at `oom_score_adj` -900 as F2
   runs it, and `tools/jetson/memory_hog.py` to the first OOM kill (it is
   the one killed; the local VLM held off). Three runs locked and three
   unlocked, alternating, each reaching `MemAvailable` <= 100 MB. Bars:
   locked, **no gap over 0.2 s** between truth samples from the hog's start
   to 10 s after its kill, in all three; unlocked (the planted check), at
   least one gap over 0.2 s in the three -- if none shows, the plant is
   recorded as not shown and criterion 4's green is reported as unproven.
5. The harness's suite passes (`test_fmea_harness.py`, `test_soak.py`);
   `/code-review` and the Thermos pass (the recorder writes recorded data).

F2's own re-run (`run.py row F2 --runs 5`, the new record) is 3.135.B's,
after this merges.
