# 3.174 Real missions: record, score and debrief

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.174; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user asked (2026-11-11, through the orchestrator) for the way real
Rover missions will be recorded, scored and debriefed once the car is ready.
3.158.A already defines what "fully autonomous" means on the car (C and D), with
a source for every fact. This section is the routine around it: what each real
mission keeps, how a batch is scored beside the simulator, and how a batch
becomes fixes. It is written, not built: docs only.

**Where it fits.** Every real mission is run in a **batch** of about 10. Batches
before 3.158.C's first counted session are **shakedowns**: scored the same way,
but they count toward no 3.158 bar. 3.158.C's and D's sessions are batches too,
and their bars stay 3.158's.

## 1. The scorecard: one row per measure, the car beside the simulator

The measures are 3.158.A's, as `evaluations/autonomy-3158/B/score.py` counts them
on simulator truth. On the car there is no truth track, so each measure names its
source, or says it cannot be measured. **Each batch's scorecard puts the car's
value beside the latest rehearsal's** (3.172.E, or the newest after it) on the same
measure. That difference is the simulator-honesty data 3.76 M1.5-M1.11 waits for.

| Measure (3.158) | Simulator (`score.py`'s field) | On the car: source | Readable? |

## 2. What each mission records, and the gaps

From what exists today:

* **3.162's report**, `recordings/what-i-saw/<mission_id>/`: the end state, job,
  target, times, the saved map's frame, `rooms_entered`, the judged frame and its
  confirmation, and one row and thumbnail per landmark.
* **3.99's intervention log**, `recordings/interventions/interventions.jsonl`:
  one line per mission end.
* **The brain's status** at the end (outcome, end reason, budget, return, tier
  stats, `log_tail`), read over HTTP before the next start.
* **3.46's inventory**, `recordings/inventory/`.
* **The robot server's `/health` and `/battery`**, read at each mission's start and
  end: refusals, the watchdog's record (3.164), the ladder and the pack.
* **The phone's video** of the run, on the user's machine.
* **The user's note**: one line per mission (below).

**Gaps, named and not built here** (3.174.B builds them):

* **G1.** No one row per mission joins these the way the rehearsal's `rows.jsonl`
  does. Scoring needs a row writer on the desktop that reads the brain and robot
  server over HTTP plus the files above.
* **G2.** No pose track per mission, so rooms entered against the house sheet and
  distance driven cannot be read.
* **G3.** The brain's and robot server's logs go to the board's journal, which is
  not kept across boots (seen 2026-11-08). They must be copied off at each
  batch's end, before any power-off, or made persistent.
* **G4.** No frames of a real mission beyond `found.jpg` and the thumbnails: the
  stream recorder serves only a teleop body (`record_walk`). So `walk_eval` and
  3.80.U's tier comparison cannot score Rover missions until frames are kept.
  They would be kept locally, never on S3 (section 6).

## 3. The batch debrief

* **A batch** is about 10 missions in one or two sessions. The user is present, as
  3.158.C requires, and writes **one line per mission**: the target and where it
  really was, what they saw, any intervention and its tag (`safety`, `stuck`,
  `wrong`, `other`), and anything odd.
* **Claude reads everything**: every report, log, status and note, and the
  video's timestamps where the user points to them. Nobody is asked to fetch logs
  (CLAUDE.md section 7).
* **One page for the user**:
  1. the scorecard (section 1), with the car, the simulator and 3.158's bar;
  2. each failure, with its cause and the evidence for it, inferred causes marked
     as inferred;
  3. what each failure goes to: an existing section or a new one (claim first, as
     always);
  4. what the batch settled (section 4).
* **A fix is proven in the simulator before the next batch**: the failure is
  reproduced there first (red), then fixed through the usual gate. A failure the
  simulator cannot reproduce is itself a 3.76 M1 finding.
* **Kept:** each batch's numbers-only rows and its page in
  `evaluations/real-missions-3174/<batch>/` (section 6 says what may not go in).

## 5. Bars, before any real mission (written 2026-11-11)

**Preconditions for the first batch**, all met and recorded:

1. The Rover's bring-up: `HARDWARE-READINESS.md` section 5, and 3.140's L0-L2
   rows met, L2 being safety on the car.
2. The board runs `dev`'s code for every merged fix that the batch's missions use
   (updated only on the user's word, `tools/jetson/README.md`).
3. The house sheet (3.158.A) is written and frozen for the batch.
4. The user is present, with Stop in reach. **No mission runs on the battery
   unattended until 3.137.C (a real power-off) and 3.137.E (the measured levels)
   are done.**
5. G1 and G3 are built or worked around, so no mission's record is lost.

**Bars for this routine** (3.158 keeps the bars for the robot):

* **R1. Every record.** For every mission in a batch: its report, its
  intervention line, its end status, `/health` and `/battery` at the start and
  end, the user's note and the copied logs. 10 of 10; a missing one is recorded
  as missing, never filled in later.
* **R2. Every measure.** Each scorecard row has a value or the word
  "unmeasurable" with its reason. No row is left blank.
* **R3. Stops.** A contact tagged `safety`, a fall, an overhang or a wrong `found`
  ends the batch at once. The next batch waits until its fix is proven in the
  simulator.
* **R4. The page.** Delivered before the next batch is started.

## 6. Privacy

Real frames, maps, inventories, landmark positions, the house sheet, the video
and the user's notes are the user's home. They stay on the robot and the desktop,
and are **never committed or uploaded unasked** (CLAUDE.md, 3.50.D). For a real
batch, `inventory_bucket`, `metrics_url` and `record_walk` stay off, as they are
by default. What is committed is numbers only: counts, outcomes, times, volts,
tags and causes in words. No position, no frame and no map; room names only as
the user allows.

## 3.174.B: the record gaps (bars written 2026-11-11, before any code)

**The build.** One observer on the desktop, `evaluations/real-missions-3174/recorder.py`,
beside the twin: a client of the robot server and the brain over HTTP, as the twin is. No line
of `robot/`, `brain/` or `control/` changes, nothing new runs on the board, and nothing in it
can move the robot (GET requests only). It uses routes that exist today:

* **G1, the row:** `/health` and `/battery` at a mission's start and end, the brain's
  `/mission/status` through the mission, its report (`/mission/reports/{id}`), and the
  intervention log's new line (a path; on the car, the batch's copied `recordings/`). One row a
  mission in `rows.jsonl`, numbers only: outcome, end reason, job, seconds, the return, refusal
  and watchdog deltas, volts at start, end and lowest, distance driven, rooms entered (from the
  track, G2), frames kept (G4), the poll's gaps, and each section 1 measure the row cannot hold
  named `unmeasurable` with its reason.
* **G2, the track:** `/world/pose` at 2 Hz into `track.jsonl` (t, x, y, heading, map id).
  Rooms entered are the track's samples against a room map in the house sheet's frame.
* **G4, the frames:** `/frame` every 2 s, written with `control.recording_routes.store_frame`
  into a `LocalWalkStore` walk per mission (split into parts at its 500-frame cap), the layout
  `walk_eval`, `label_assist` and 3.80.U's `make_sets.py` read.
* **Where it writes:** a batch directory outside the repository (default
  `~/picar-kept/real-missions/<batch>/`); it refuses one inside a git work tree (section 6).
  Only `rows.jsonl`'s numbers may be copied into `evaluations/real-missions-3174/<batch>/`,
  and the row holds no position, frame or map.
* **G3 is written up only** (it changes the board; the user's go): the choice between a
  persistent journal on the board and a read-only copy at each batch's end, with what each
  costs. Nothing is run on the board.

**Measured** on the live local stack (`home_first_floor_stairs`, saved map, 3.158.B's harness
and stand-ins, as 3.173.C), with the recorder pointed at it exactly as at the car: a batch of

### G3: the board's logs across boots (written up only; the board is the user's go)

**What is true:** the brain's and robot server's logs go to the board's journal through
`picar-stack`, and on 2026-11-08 the journal held nothing from before the last boot, so it is
kept in memory only (journald's `Storage=auto` with no `/var/log/journal`: inferred from that,
not read off the board here). A mission that ends in a reboot or a power-off loses exactly the
log that would say why.

**Option 1, a persistent journal (a change to the board).** A drop-in
`/etc/systemd/journald.conf.d/picar.conf` with `Storage=persistent` and a cap
(`SystemMaxUse=500M`, say), then a journald restart. It needs sudo on the board, so the
user's go, and it lands beside 3.136.R's sudo revert. Costs, inferred, not measured:
* a few MB of NVMe writes per hour of INFO logging, negligible against its endurance;
* journald's CPU, already paid;
* a volatile journal's RAM, given back. This is a small gain against 3.81's 0.5 GB memory
  floor, unmeasured.

What it buys: the log of a crash, a brownout or 3.137.C's power-off survives the boot that
follows it. Neither option below does that.

**Option 2, a read-only copy at each batch's end (no change to the board).** The desktop runs
`journalctl -u picar-stack -u picar-local-vlm --since <batch start> -o short-iso` over ssh,
and copies `recordings/interventions/` and `recordings/what-i-saw/` into the batch directory,
before any power-off. Costs:
* anything that ends in an unplanned reboot is lost before it can be copied;
* a long batch may outrun the volatile journal's size cap (journald's runtime default, a
  share of `/run`; not read here), and its oldest lines rotate out.

**Recommendation:** both. Option 1 so a fault's log survives; option 2 as the routine,
because the logs have to reach the desktop for the debrief anyway. Until the user says yes to
option 1, every batch uses option 2, and a mission that ends in a reboot records its logs as
**missing** (R1), never as reconstructed. The copy step is not built in 3.174.B: it reads the
board, and is run and checked at 3.174.C's first batch.

**On the car (the orchestrator, 2026-11-11).** The frame interval is a setting
(`--frame-every-s`, default 2 s; 0 keeps none). At the car the recorder reaches the robot and
brain through `service/tunnel/` with `APP_SHARED_SECRET` set (read from the environment, never
a flag), as the twin does. B6 is the check that its polling does not load the robot, measured
at the default rate.

**Review (Sonnet, round 1, before the run).** Four fixed, red first where the recorder changed:
1. With the brain unreachable for good, or its end missed in an outage, the loop never ended
   and no row was written. A row is now written after `STATUS_LOST_S` (120 s) with no status
   of the mission, `row_end: status_lost`, outcome None (the test hung before the fix).
2. A frame write that raised (a full disk), or a cut intervention line, ended the thread with
   no row. Now each is counted (`frame_errors`, kind `unreadable`).
3. Distance split legs on the map id only; a change of frame id in one session (anchoring on
   the saved map) now starts a leg too.
4. B1 read a None value as an absent field; `end_state` of a fault and `ret_state` with no return
   are values. B1 now checks the key, plus readings named `missing`.

**B4's reading (written before the run):** the bar stays as written, every (mission, room)
pair of the house's 13 rooms. The stricter share, over the rooms the track or the truth
entered, is reported beside it with no bar. Noted, not changed: a refused start leaves the
recorder thread polling until the process exits; the truth track is sampled at about 1 Hz
against the recorder's 2 Hz, so B3 carries a small bias on curved paths.

**Smoke (one find, the rec arm, before the run):** `found` in 126 s, the track 18.61 m against
truth's 18.61 m, 64 frames, coverage 1.0, no gap, recorder joined; its data set aside, not
counted.
