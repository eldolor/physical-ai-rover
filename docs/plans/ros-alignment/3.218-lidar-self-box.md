# 3.218 The lidar's own mast: a measured self box for forward moves (reverse stays blind)

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.218; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** On the Rover's first wheels-up check through the twin (3.140,
2026-11-26) every FORWARD and REVERSE was refused: "distance=0.0cm"
(`scan_footprint`) and "rear clearance=0.0cm" (`scan_rear`). The D500 sees
the pan-tilt mast directly behind it: returns at 2.3-14 cm over the arc
-180..-134 and +136..+180 degrees (body, 0 ahead, + right), in all 40 of
40 scans (`evaluations/lidar-self-box-3218/selfhits-20261126.json`; only
the returns under 30 cm are kept, never the room). In the body frame they
lie in x -6.1..+2.2 cm, y -9.7..+7.5 cm: inside the chassis outline
(half-length 12.65, half-width 11.55), so the corridor reads "the body is
on it" (0.0). Moving the camera's USB cable did not change them.

**Decided by the user, 2026-11-26** (each its own question):
* Build a self box in the safety layer, properly, not strip the returns
  from the scan. Stripping them would make the rear read CLEAR where the
  mast in fact hides it: a missing return means "clear" in the scan
  format, and M3's rule is that unobserved is never clear.
* Turning in place is judged on what the lidar sees: the self box is
  ignored and the blind rear arc counts as clear beyond the mast. The
  accepted risk: an object right behind a rear corner that the Rover has
  not seen. (The other options were: refuse turns near the unseen arc;
  raise the lidar above the mast.)

**What it is.**
* `safety.lidar_self_box_cm: [x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max]` (body frame,
  cm, x ahead, y left), or `ROBOT_LIDAR_SELF_BOX_CM="x0,x1,y0,y1"`. Unset
  (the default, and the simulator): nothing changes.
* FORWARD: returns inside the box are not obstacles to the corridor (the
  mast is behind the lidar and moves with the body).
* REVERSE: unchanged in effect: still refused while the box's returns are
  there, now named `astern_occluded` ("the Rover cannot see behind
  itself"), not a false obstacle distance.
* Pivots: no change needed: a return inside the chassis outline never
  "closes" under rotation (3.19's rule), so the mast never blocked turns.
  Checked by a test, not assumed.
* The box must lie inside the chassis outline with at least 0.5 cm to
  every edge; the robot server refuses to start otherwise.

**The box for this Rover:** the measured extents plus 1.0 cm each way:
x -7.1..+3.2, y -10.7..+8.5 cm (0.85 cm inside the right edge).

**Criteria (written 2026-11-26, before building; CLAUDE.md section 7).**

1. **Every recorded self return is inside the box**, and a scan made of
   only those returns gives FORWARD no target: all 92 bins, at both their
   median and their maximum range.
2. **The box is inside the outline** by at least 0.5 cm on every side, and
   a box that is not is refused at start.
3. **Nothing else changes.** For 10,000 seeded random scans with no
   return inside the box, `footprint_clearance(+1)`, `footprint_clearance(-1)`,
   `reverse_clearance()` and `pivot_scale()` give identical numbers with
   and without the box.
4. **Reverse stays refused** with the mast's returns present, reported as
   `astern_occluded`.
5. **Turns are not blocked by the mast**: `pivot_scale()` is 1.0 for a
   scan of only the recorded self returns, with or without the box.
6. **Default off**: the fast tier passes; the frozen references
   (`tests/pivot_reference.py`, `tests/vet_reference.py`) are unchanged.
7. **On the Rover, wheels up, the user present (step B, the user's go):**
   D-pad FORWARD runs with nothing ahead; FORWARD is refused with an
   object 10 cm ahead of the front bumper; REVERSE is refused as
   `astern_occluded`; a 15-degree turn each way runs.

Progress paired with 2-6: criterion 7's forward moves and turns.
