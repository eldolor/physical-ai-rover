# 3.157 Explore's sightings from real perception: bearing and sighting rate on recorded walks

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.157; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** Full autonomy (the user, 2026-11-06) needs explore to find the
target from what a real camera gives. Plain `explore` reads the frame's
`detections`, which only the simulator provides; under the tiered policy
with `tier_search: explore` (3.80.G) explore's sightings come from the
tiers' perception instead (`sight_from: perception`, `brain/explore.py`
`_hits`): YOLOE + CLIP's detected bearing less the camera's pan, or the
local VLM's located yes. That path is built and judged only on the
simulator's frames (3.80.G, 3.80.O). On real frames nothing has measured
what explore would receive: how often a target in view yields a sighting,
how far its bearing is off, how often a sighting appears with the target
out of view, and whether the pan correction holds on a real camera. 3.92
found perception silent on 20.4% of in-view frames; that is per frame,
not what explore's close-in and arrival see over a stretch.

**Scope.** Offline and free first: the labelled rig walks (3.92's 2,261
frames, `labels.json`) replayed through the tiers' perception and
`_hits()` as explore calls it, scored against the labels (target in view,
its bearing where labelled). Then the same harness on the Rover's own
frames (3.80.U's walks). No model call is paid; the local VLM runs only
where it is free.

**Criteria (written in step A, before measuring; CLAUDE.md section 7).**
Step A writes the bars here: at least the sighting rate on in-view frames
and over stretches of k frames, the bearing error (p50/p90, degrees), false
sightings on out-of-view frames, and what explore does with them in a
replay (close-in started on the right object), each with its bar and the
frame set pinned before measuring.

## 3.157.A: the frame set, the harness and the bars (written 2026-11-07, before measuring)

**The frame set, pinned.** 3.92's 2,261 frames: the 21 walks with an
adjudicated `labels.json` (535 in view, 1,726 out), in `common.items()`
order. Perception and the local VLM are NOT re-run: the records are 3.92's
own, read as they are -- `evaluations/tier-calibration-392/perception.jsonl`
(the shipped pipeline, YOLOE-11s-seg crops -> CLIP RN50, one CPU, P24: no
GPU row is mixed in) and `qwen-VE.jsonl` (VE on the board, the live
`/identity` question with `box: true`). Free; no model call of any kind.

**What explore receives, per frame.** Each frame's records are put in the
scene shape the tiered policy hands explore (`_perception` with status,
bearing and `pan_deg` 0; `_tier.local_landed` with the local answer and its
body bearing) and read by `brain/explore.py` `_hits()` itself, under
`sight_from: perception`:
* **perception's sighting:** status `detected` (P >= 0.8); bearing as the
  pipeline computes it, `(box centre / width - 0.5) * hfov`, hfov 66
  (`perceive.py`'s default);
* **the local VLM's sighting:** VE's score at least `LOCAL_YES_AT` (0.5)
  with a box; bearing as `tiered.py` computes it from the box's fractions
  (`brain.local_vlm.model_size`).
* The phone on the rig never pans, so the pan correction is exercised only
  at pan 0: **its real-camera check is owed to 3.157.C** (the Rover).
* Limits, recorded: every frame is treated as if the local VLM answered it
  (live, VE answers one question in flight at ~1.2 s, so the local source is
  an upper bound); no remembered no (3.91.C `_refused`) is replayed (it
  needs the mission's motion); hfov 66 is the Rover camera's default, not
  the phone's, so degrees are "at 66" -- the pixel error is what carries.

**Bars, on perception's sighting alone** (what explore gets every frame;
perception + local is recorded beside each, as the upper bound):

1. **B1, in view, per frame:** at least **70%** of the 535 in-view frames
   give a sighting.
2. **B2, in view, over a stretch:** of every run of k consecutive in-view
   frames within a walk (k = 3 and k = 5, sliding), at least **90%** (k =
   3) and **95%** (k = 5) hold at least one sighting.
3. **B3, bearing:** on 3.80.I's S1 (60 in-view frames drawn uniformly
   from the 535, seed 380, never from detections), the target's horizontal
   extent is labelled by viewing each frame (a 10% grid drawn, the
   detection's box never shown); truth = its centre. Over S1 frames with a
   sighting: |bearing - truth| p50 at most **3 deg** (`ARRIVAL_CENTRE_DEG`)
   and p90 at most **6 deg**, both at hfov 66. A frame whose target cannot
   be placed is dropped and counted. Coverage (sighted frames of 60)
   recorded.
4. **B4, out of view:** at most **2%** of the 1,726 out-of-view frames give
   a sighting, and at most **1%** of out-of-view runs of k = 5 hold two or
   more.
5. **B5, replay, close-in starts on the right object:** walking each walk
   in order, explore would start its close-in on the first sighting (one
   hit, ranged by the lidar; the rig has no scan, so the close-in itself is
   not replayed). In at least **19 of 21** walks that first sighting is on
   an in-view frame AND its bearing is within **6 deg** of the target's
   centre on that frame (labelled as in B3, blind to the box). A walk with
   no sighting at all counts as a miss.

Recorded, no bar: each rate per object; the same numbers for perception +
local and for the local VLM alone; the first false sighting's frame per
walk.

**Harness:** `evaluations/explore-sight-3157/` (step B): `score.py` reads
the two record files and the labels through 3.92's `common.items()`, builds
each frame's scene and calls `ExploreAgent._hits`; `labels-bearing.json`
holds B3/B5's centres, written before `score.py` first runs.
