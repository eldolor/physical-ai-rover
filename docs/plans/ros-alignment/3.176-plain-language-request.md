# 3.176 A find asked in plain words: the target, how it looks and a room hint

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.176; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** (the user, 2026-11-12: features of similar products and projects worth
building while the Rover waits on Waveshare.) The hobby project closest to this
one (Zero-Shot-VLN-ROS2) puts a language model in front of the search: it turns
what the person typed into a goal the detector and the navigator can use. Here
the target text goes to the detector as typed. Three known costs:

* a multi-word target once blinded the label gates (3.76 L1);
* a person who knows the room ("my water bottle, probably in the kitchen")
  cannot say so, while the `rooms` goal policy has a slot for exactly that
  (3.96's room prior; 3.116 measured what labels are worth);
* with 3.160's names, "in the kitchen" can name a room on the saved map.

**Scope.**
* One step at mission start: the request becomes `{target, detector prompts,
  attributes, room hint}`. The target stays what the identity check is asked;
  the hint only orders where explore looks, and never ends a search early.
* **A free rule-based parser first** (a room named after "in", "near", "by";
  colour and size words): no model, works offline (3.49).
* A model arm second (the cloud, or the local VLM if it can), behind a config
  key, off by default, one call per mission, bounded like `/room`. Its
  evaluation costs money, so it waits on the user's go with an estimate.
* The twin's Find box takes the sentence and shows what was understood before
  Start, so a wrong reading is caught by the person.

**Criteria (written in step A, before building; CLAUDE.md section 7).** At
least:
* on a written set of requests (pinned before measuring; hints right, wrong and
  absent): the parser's target and room exact-match rates, with bars;
* in the simulator with a right hint: median goals and seconds against no hint
  (progress), finds held; with a **wrong** hint: finds within a bar of no hint
  and a bound on the seconds lost (stability);
* a plain one-word target behaves exactly as today (0 differences);
* offline: the rule arm works with every connection refused;
* phone-size evidence of the understood-request line.
