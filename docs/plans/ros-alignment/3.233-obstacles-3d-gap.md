# 3.233 What a 3-D house shows that the 2-D sim's obstacle checks miss

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.233; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Status: opened 2026-11-28; amended the same day (amendment 1, below) before anything was measured.**

**Why.** The user asked (2026-11-28) why the simulator is not MuJoCo or
another 3-D simulator, and then: what if the simulator's main job is
obstacle avoidance? For that job the 2-D grid's blind spot is height:
* the lidar sees one plane, 12 cm up (`LIDAR_Z_M`, 3.27);
* each object has one height, below or above that plane (3.74);
* the OAK-D floor band sees "any height" but models no elevation
  (`docs/simulator/ARCHITECTURE.md`, 3.74 trade-off).

So nothing in the sim can be **above the lidar's plane and below the
Rover's top**: a bed or sofa frame with open space under it, a low table's
apron, a chair's rungs. On a real Rover the lidar passes under such a part
and the body strikes it. The user chose (2026-11-28) to **measure this gap
before building anything**: this section decides, on data, whether the
sim's sensor casts should move to 3-D geometry.

**Not in this section, and where it lives:**
* Slip, braking distance and scrub: 3.73 (MuJoCo fitted to the Rover).
* Climbing or stalling on a mat edge or threshold: 3.230.
* Glass, mirrors and black surfaces under the lidar: on the Rover only
  (3.42 criterion 7, 3.74 part B). No sim result speaks to them.
* Photoreal frames: Isaac Sim stays not adopted (3.73).

**What stays as decided.** One simulator (`docs/simulator/ARCHITECTURE.md`).
If C opens, 3-D geometry goes *inside* `GridWorld`'s sensor casts; no
second simulator is run beside it. Contact is judged on ground truth
(geometry), never on the readings the veto uses (CLAUDE.md section 6).

## Terms

* **The body band:** heights from the Rover's underside clearance up to its
  tallest point, both from the xacro (`picar.urdf.xacro`; the pan joint is
  0.168 m off the floor, and several values are `[PLACEHOLDER]` until
  measured on the car). A, below, writes the two numbers down.
* **A hidden hazard:** a part of a floor-standing object that lies inside
  the body band, over floor the Rover could otherwise drive, while the
  object has **nothing at the lidar's plane** (12 cm) above that floor. The
  lidar cannot see it; the body can hit it.

## Amendment 1 (the user, 2026-11-28, before any step was claimed)

**A curated catalogue of about 100 household obstacles, overlaid on the
simulator as shapes in metres with a height range each, replaces the search
for licensed 3-D meshes of ProcTHOR's furniture.** Why:
* ProcTHOR's 10,000 `train` houses hold only **34 floor-standing types**
  (chair, dining table, bed, dresser, bin, toilet, sofa, floor lamp, ...)
  as bounding boxes, which cannot show an open underside; 3.74 adds 14
  small floor hazards. A hand-built catalogue with measured dimensions
  covers what neither has.
* **Every house is 30 cm cells** (`CELL_M`, `FOOTPRINT_CELL_M`). Placed as
  cells, a 1 cm cable, a 2 cm chair leg and a 30 cm box are the same block:
  the body keeps clear of a thin leg it would really clip, and the lidar
  ranges a block it might really miss. So the obstacles are shapes, not
  cells; the user rejected the cells-only shortcut for that reason.
* It is still one simulator: the overlay lives inside `GridWorld`'s casts
  and contact check (`docs/simulator/ARCHITECTURE.md`).

## Step A: catalogue, body band, placement

* **A1. The catalogue**, `evaluations/obstacles-3d-233/catalogue.yaml`:
  about 100 entries. Sources, in order: ProcTHOR's 34 floor-standing types;
  3.74's 14 floor hazards; the common indoor-robot troublemakers (bed and
  sofa frames open underneath, chair and stool rungs, pedestal bases, floor
  lamp bases, cords and cables, door stops, baby and pet gates, an open
  dishwasher door, laundry piles, glass tables, mirrors, ...). Each entry:
  * a shape: `box`, `cylinder`, `legs` (n posts plus an optional slab on
    them), `slab` (a plate between two heights), `line` (a cable);
  * its dimensions and its height range, bottom to top, in metres;
  * a **category**: `solid`, `legged`, `overhang`, `thin`, `low`,
    `reflective`;
  * a **source** for its numbers (a product sheet, a standard, a tape
    measure); none invented. An entry with no source is left out.
* **A2. The body band** from the xacro: underside clearance and tallest
  point, each with its `[CAD]` / `[PLACEHOLDER]` mark. C reports at those
  values and at +/-3 cm on the top.
* **A3. The placement rule**, seeded and never by hand: per house, k
  obstacles drawn from the catalogue (every category represented), placed
  on free floor along the routes today's sweep actually drives (from 3.66's
  sweep trajectories), each at least one chassis width from a door gap so
  no house is sealed. A placement that cuts a room off is redrawn by rule.
* **A4. `reflective` entries** carry the sensor effect as a declared model
  (the lidar returns nothing, or a return at the mirror's image distance),
  marked as an assumption: C's numbers for them test how the safety layer
  handles the dropout, not how often the real D500 misses glass.

**A's criterion:** A1-A4 written and committed before B is claimed; at
least 90 sourced entries, every category with at least 5.

## Step B: the overlay (criteria, written before building)

1. **Sensing by height.** The lidar's plane (`LIDAR_Z_M`) and the level depth
   row see an obstacle only where their height cuts its height range; the
   floor band sees any obstacle inside its range, as today (3.74). Pinned
   by a test per shape and per category, against hand-computed geometry.
2. **Contact by height.** A strike is the chassis' swept footprint (ground
   truth, `tests/chassis_fit.py`) overlapping a shape whose height range
   overlaps the body band. Judged on geometry only.
3. **No regression with it empty.** With no overlay obstacles every existing
   pinned number holds exactly (the frozen references in CLAUDE.md section
   6 included), and the full suite passes.
4. **Cost.** The 100-house sweep's wall-clock with the overlay is measured
   against today's and recorded; above 3x is reported to the user before C.

## Step C: the sweep and the verdict

**Set:** the first 100 usable `val` houses by index (3.66's rule), each with
A3's placement. **Runs:** 3.66's `explore` sweep with `FakeNav`, its seeds
and step budget, two sensor cases:
* **C-lidar:** the lidar and the level depth row only (the veto on the Rover
  today: 3.222's floor band has not passed);
* **C-band:** plus the floor band as the sim models it.

**Metrics** (written 2026-11-28, before measuring):

1. **Strike rate (the decision metric):** the share of missions with at
   least one strike, per case, **and per category**.
2. **Near misses:** the closest ground-truth approach to each obstacle,
   per category (a miss by 1 cm is reported as such, not as a pass).
3. **Progress, paired (CLAUDE.md section 7 rule 4):** arrival rate and rooms
   reached, against the same 100 houses with no overlay. A strike rate that
   falls because missions stop early is not a pass.

**Verdict, on C-lidar's strike rate, per category** (C-band beside it):
* **>= 5% of missions strike that category:** D changes the safety layer
  (or its sensors) for it.
* **0 strikes:** that category is cleared in the sim; no change.
* **Between:** recorded with every strike's house, obstacle and height; the
  user decides (one question, recommendation first).

**Limits stated now.** The houses are generated and the catalogue is
generic, not the user's rooms; a pass does not clear the user's house, and a
fail is still a reason to act. `reflective` results test the response to a
declared dropout, not the D500 (A4).
