# 3.233 What a 3-D house shows that the 2-D sim's obstacle checks miss

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.233; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Status: opened 2026-11-28; criteria written, nothing measured.**

**Why.** The user asked (2026-11-28) why the simulator is not MuJoCo or
another 3-D simulator, and then: what if the simulator's main job is
obstacle avoidance? For that job the 2-D grid's blind spot is height:
* the lidar sees one plane, 12 cm up (`LIDAR_Z_M`, 3.27);
* each object has one height, below or above that plane (3.74);
* the OAK-D floor band sees "any height" but models no elevation
  (`docs/simulator/ARCHITECTURE.md`, 3.74 trade-off).

So nothing in the sim can be **above the lidar's plane and below the
Rover's top**: a bed or sofa frame with open space under it, a low table's
apron, a chair's rungs. On a real Rover the lidar passes under such a part
and the body strikes it. The user chose (2026-11-28) to **measure this gap
before building anything**: this section decides, on data, whether the
sim's sensor casts should move to 3-D geometry.

**Not in this section, and where it lives:**
* Slip, braking distance and scrub: 3.73 (MuJoCo fitted to the Rover).
* Climbing or stalling on a mat edge or threshold: 3.230.
* Glass, mirrors and black surfaces under the lidar: on the Rover only
  (3.42 criterion 7, 3.74 part B). No sim result speaks to them.
* Photoreal frames: Isaac Sim stays not adopted (3.73).

**What stays as decided.** One simulator (`docs/simulator/ARCHITECTURE.md`).
If C opens, 3-D geometry goes *inside* `GridWorld`'s sensor casts; no
second simulator is run beside it. Contact is judged on ground truth
(geometry), never on the readings the veto uses (CLAUDE.md section 6).

## Terms

* **The body band:** heights from the Rover's underside clearance up to its
  tallest point, both from the xacro (`picar.urdf.xacro`; the pan joint is
  0.168 m off the floor, and several values are `[PLACEHOLDER]` until
  measured on the car). A, below, writes the two numbers down.
* **A hidden hazard:** a part of a floor-standing object that lies inside
  the body band, over floor the Rover could otherwise drive, while the
  object has **nothing at the lidar's plane** (12 cm) above that floor. The
  lidar cannot see it; the body can hit it.

## Step A: geometry, licence, body band

Writes down, before B measures:
* **A1. The source.** Where per-object 3-D shape comes from. ProcTHOR's
  asset database gives bounding boxes only, which cannot show an open
  underside. Candidates: the AI2-THOR asset meshes, or a per-category
  profile (a bed's frame height, a sofa's skirt) measured from meshes once.
  **Its licence is checked and recorded first;** a non-commercial source
  is not used (the user's rule, 3.122). If no usable source has shape below
  the box, A says so, and B runs on boxes with that limit written into its
  verdict.
* **A2. The body band** from the xacro: underside clearance and tallest
  point, each with its `[CAD]` / `[PLACEHOLDER]` mark. B reports its
  numbers at those values and at +/-3 cm on the top, because the top is a
  placeholder.
* **A3. B's harness**, kept in `evaluations/obstacles-3d-233/`, not `sim/`:
  how a house is built with hidden hazards absent from the sensors (as the
  real lidar would see it) while the contact check still knows them. If
  that needs a change under `sim/`, A records it and B's step row is
  amended before it is claimed.

**A's criterion:** A1-A3 are written in this file, with the licence's text
cited, before B is claimed.

## Step B: the measurement

**Set:** the first 100 usable `val` houses by index (3.66's rule; chosen by
rule, never by hand). **Runs:** 3.66's `explore` sweep with `FakeNav`, its
seeds and step budget, two sensor cases:
* **B-lidar:** the lidar and the level depth row only (the veto on the Rover
  today: 3.222's floor band has not passed);
* **B-band:** plus the floor band as the sim models it (optimistic: it sees
  any height).

**Metrics** (written 2026-11-28, before measuring):

1. **Prevalence:** hidden hazards per house, and the share of houses with
   at least one, from geometry alone. Reported, no bar: it says how much of
   a house the grid misrepresents.
2. **Strike rate (the decision metric):** the share of missions in which
   the Rover's swept footprint (`tests/chassis_fit.py`'s chassis, ground
   truth) overlaps a hidden hazard's footprint at least once. Per case.
3. **Progress, paired (CLAUDE.md section 7 rule 4):** arrival rate and rooms
   reached, against the same 100 houses with today's grid. Hidden hazards
   now read as free space, so a strike rate that falls because missions
   stop early is not a pass.

**Verdict, on B-lidar's strike rate** (B-band reported beside it):
* **>= 5% of missions strike a hidden hazard:** the gap is real; C builds
  3-D sensor casts.
* **0 strikes in 100 houses, with prevalence > 0:** the safety layer and
  the houses' layouts keep the Rover clear; C closes, nothing is built.
* **Between:** recorded with every strike's house, object and height; the
  user decides C (one question, recommendation first).
* If A1 found no shape below the box, every verdict carries that: a bed
  modelled as a solid box can show no open underside, so a low strike rate
  then means "not shown", not "absent".

**Limits stated now.** ProcTHOR's furniture is generated, not the user's
house. A pass here does not clear the user's rooms; a fail is still a
reason to build, because the safety layer cannot see what the geometry
says is there.
