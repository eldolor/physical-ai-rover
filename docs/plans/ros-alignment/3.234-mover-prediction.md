# 3.234 Predicting where people and pets will be: a short-horizon mover tracker in the safety veto

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.234; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** The user, 2026-11-28, asked whether the rover predicts what will
happen next on a mission or in frontier exploration, then asked for this
section. The answer was: only over a wheel-loop's horizon. The pivot
look-ahead (`PIVOT_LOOKAHEAD_S`, 50 ms), aged readings (`_aged()`, 3.36), the
swept corridor (3.18) and the drop guard (0.1 s) all predict where the
**chassis** will be. Nothing predicts where anything **else** will be.

**What exists today (read at `dev` `61cbebd`).**
* `robot/safety.py` stops for what the scan shows now. A person or pet is
  just more returns. There is no closing-speed term, which
  `PLAN-onboard-perception.md`'s "Dynamic obstacles" item already noted.
* explore treats a person in a doorway through time, not prediction:
  `RetryBook` (`brain/frontier.py`) sets the goal aside and tries again
  later.
* **The simulator cannot test this.** `sim/movers.py` (3.30) hops whole
  30 cm cells, and a mover never steps closer to the robot
  (`MOVER_KEEPOUT_M`). 3.30 states this fidelity limit itself: "this cannot
  test a pet darting at it (a continuous disc in the ray caster is the
  upgrade)".

**Why a static stop line is not enough** (arithmetic, not a measurement).
`T_REACT_S` is at least `BOARD_HEARTBEAT_S + VERB_PERIOD_S` (about 0.3 s).
A person at 1.2 m/s covers about 0.36 m in that time, and a pet at 2.5 m/s
about 0.75 m. Both are more than the 20 cm stop line (`min_distance_cm`).
Within the reaction time, the line cannot tell a person walking into the
corridor from one standing beside it.

**What this section may and may not do.**
* **Prediction only shrinks clearance; it never grows it.** A predicted
  mover becomes extra points for the swept-corridor check (rule 4). It can
  add a stop or a slow-down, and can never permit a move today's veto
  refuses. With no track present, the veto answers to the bit as today.
* **Its home is the robot server, not ROS** (rule 3: a sensor that feeds a
  veto stays outside). The input is `robot/lidar_ld19.py`'s scan at 10 Hz.
  The camera may name a track in a later section, never in this one.
* **Robot-caused only.** A mover that walks into a stopped robot is not a
  failure the robot can fix. The metric is 3.30's: how close the robot gets
  through its **own** motion, on ground truth (`tests/footprint_sweep.py`'s
  rule: share no code with the thing judged).
* **Off by default** behind a config key in `config/robot.yaml`, kept in
  step with any brain config it touches (section 6).
* A risky change (the safety path): `/code-review` and the Thermos bug
  pass on every code commit.

**The gate in A.** A measures today's code with the new movers. If no
robot-caused close approach happens, there is nothing to fix: B-D do not
start, and the section closes recorded, the user's word on it.

## Criteria (written before building or measuring; CLAUDE.md section 7)

**A. The simulator learns to approach** (records plus a gate)
* **A1.** An opt-in mover mode (`SIM_MOVERS` value to be named in A):
  continuous positions, a disc in the ray caster, and paths that cross the
  robot's own route at 0.5, 1.2 and 2.5 m/s, approaching as well as
  passing. Today's movers and their pinned tests are unchanged.
* **A2.** Ground truth: the robot's closest approach to each mover's disc
  that the robot's own motion caused (the share of the gap closed by the
  robot's velocity). Instrument shares no code with `robot/safety.py`,
  `get_scan()` or the renderer.
* **A3, the gate.** Today's veto, the four houses x 3 starts x 3 speeds,
  `drive: verbs` and `drive: ros` (FakeNav in process): the count of
  robot-caused approaches inside the 20 cm line. **0 means B-D do not
  start.** Recorded with the arrival rate and median mission time, which
  are C's baseline.

**B. The tracker, offline** (on A's recorded scans, under `SIM_LIDAR=fake`'s
real timing)
* **B1, false tracks.** The four houses with no movers, the robot driving:
  at most **1 false moving track (> 0.3 m/s) per 10 minutes** of driving.
* **B2, accuracy.** Movers at 0.5-2.5 m/s: velocity error median
  **<= 0.15 m/s**, p90 **<= 0.35 m/s**; a track within **3 scans** (0.3 s)
  of a mover coming into the lidar's view.
* **B3, cost.** p90 **<= 5 ms a scan** on the Jetson with the full stack;
  0 late wheel-loop ticks.
* The horizon (how far ahead a track is projected) is chosen in B from
  B2's error and `T_REACT_S`, written in an amendment before C runs.

**C. In the veto** (behind the key, in process, then one live run)
* **C1, safety.** A3's sweep with the tracker on: robot-caused approaches
  inside the 20 cm line **0**, and at most **half** of A3's count inside
  30 cm.
* **C2, progress.** Arrival rate within **2 points** of A3's; median
  mission time no more than **+10 %**; time stopped for a predicted
  (not present) mover reported.
* **C3, nothing else changes.** With no movers: every frozen reference and
  `tests/footprint_sweep.py` sweep unchanged to the bit (pinned).
* **C4.** One mission against the live local stack through the brain's
  HTTP API with the new movers, judged by its logs.

**D. On the Rover** (the user beside it, at nav2's 0.20 m/s)
* **D1.** 10 minutes driving in an empty room: **0** stops caused by a
  predicted mover.
* **D2.** The user crosses the robot's route at walking pace, 10 trials:
  the robot is stopped before the user's path in **>= 9 of 10**, and
  never closes on the user through its own motion.

### Amendment 1 (2026-11-28, before A3's sweep ran): what A1-A3 measure

Written after the instrument was built and tried on single missions, and
before the sweep whose numbers judge A3.

* **A1's mode is `SIM_MOVERS=approach:<m/s>[:<seed>]`**, in any house
  (`sim.maps.build_movers`). One `sim.movers.DiscMover`: a 0.15 m disc
  that walks straight legs at the speed given. The legs alternate:
  * aimed at the robot's centre (approaching);
  * across its route 0.5 m ahead of it, within 45 degrees of square to its
    heading (passing).

  Each leg carries on past the aim as far again. A leg starts at a seeded
  free spot 1.0-3.0 m from the robot with clear floor to the aim (someone
  coming round a corner), and the disc stands 1 s between legs. It never
  comes within 1 cm of the chassis (it stops there and the leg ends), so
  contact is the robot's doing or nobody's.
* **Where the disc is seen.** In every ray cast that takes `solid` (the
  scan, the depth grid, the drop cast, collision), through
  `renderer.SolidWithDiscs`. With no disc mover, `solid_cells` is the plain
  frozenset, so today's movers and every pinned number are unchanged.
  Limits: the camera does not draw it (`_visible_objects` reads
  `objects`), nor does `MockWorld`'s map (its own ray march, cells only);
  the split body (`SIM_MOTOR_BOARD=fake`, 3.36) and `SIM_LIDAR=fake` do not
  carry it and the factory refuses the pair (code review), so it runs in
  process and in the one-process robot server only; speed is capped at
  5 m/s; the
  starter house's 30 cm doors cannot pass a 30 cm disc.
* **A2 is travel-to-contact, not the Euclidean gap** (3.18's and 3.30's
  metric). On single missions the gap counted two things that are not
  approaches: driving past a person beside the route (today's rule allows
  that beside a wall too), and a pivot's sub-millimetre nudge while a pet
  ran in. `tests/approach_sweep.py` splits every body step against the
  disc where it stood when the step began. After a step that translated
  the robot, `T` is how far the chassis could carry on that way before
  touching the disc.
  * A **robot-caused approach inside a line** is an entry in which the
    robot translated at least **1 cm** in steps that ended with `T` under
    the line. The entry re-arms when the true gap is back above the line
    plus 10 cm.
  * Pivots are not counted (3.19's guard has its own metric).
  * Each approach also records the robot's share of all closing since the
    gap was last 1 m.
* **A3's sweep:**
  * Houses and starts: `starter_house`, `scaled_house`,
    `home_first_floor`, `complex_house`; 3 seeded starts each, with a
    target the house holds.
  * Speeds 0.5, 1.2 and 2.5 m/s.
  * `drive: verbs` is the `frontier` policy (150 steps). `drive: ros` is
    `explore` with `tests/drop_sweep.py`'s VettedNav (nav2's 0.2 m/s through
    `vet_wheel_velocity()`).
  * 600 sim seconds a mission; 72 missions. The gate counts approaches
    inside 20 cm over all 72.

### Amendment 2 (2026-11-28, before B was built or measured): how B records and judges

* **Honest timing without the split body.** A's factory refuses disc
  movers under `SIM_LIDAR=fake` (the pty fake cannot see a disc). B
  records instead, in process, every body step's time, true pose and disc
  (A3's missions, same seeds, by `tests/approach_sweep.py`'s mission
  loop), then **replays the timeline through the fake lidar's own timing
  model**: `sim.fake_lidar.Emitter` casts point k at `t0 + k / 4500 s` from
  the lidar pose and disc interpolated linearly to that instant (a disc
  that jumps to a new leg is held at its last sample until the jump), the
  packets are LD19-encoded and decoded by `robot.lidar_ld19.Ld19Scanner`,
  each packet fed at its last point's instant (`tests/footprint_sweep.py`'s
  `_LidarTimed`). The tracker reads exactly what `get_scan()` would give
  on the car: 360 nearest-return bins and `capture_unix` (the revolution's
  midpoint). Limits: odometry is the true pose at `capture_unix` (no wheel
  slip; the Rover's odometry is D's to judge); offline, so the scan is
  never late as on a loaded server (B3 is that).
* **The tracker** (`robot/mover_tracker.py`, imported by nothing in the
  veto in B): bins to points, segments by range jump, clusters of person
  or pet size not occluded at an edge, nearest-neighbour association in the
  odometry frame, velocity as a least-squares slope over a short window.
  A track is **moving** when it has 3 observations and speed > 0.3 m/s.
* **Development and measurement use different starts.** The tracker's
  constants are set on starts seeded `seed=1` (`approach_sweep.starts(...,
  seed=1)`); B1 and B2 are measured once on A3's starts (`seed=0`).
* **B1:** the four houses x 3 starts x both drives with no mover (24
  missions, 600 sim seconds each). A false track is a distinct track id
  that is ever moving. Driving time is the wheel-loop periods in which the
  body translated or turned. Bar: at most 1 per 10 minutes of it.
* **B2:** A3's 72 missions. The disc is **in view** on a revolution when
  at least 2 of its points (returns within 2 cm of the disc's edge at their
  instant) are in it; a visit starts at the first revolution in view after
  2 out of view (or the leg's start). **Time to first track** is the count
  of revolutions from the visit's first, inclusive, to the first with a
  moving track within 0.4 m of the disc's centre; the bar is <= 3 for every
  visit lasting at least 3 revolutions, reported as the share that meet
  it and its p90 (a visit that never gets a track counts as failing).
  **Velocity error** is |v_track - v_disc| (vectors, m/s) on every
  revolution a moving track lies within 0.4 m of the disc's centre, v_disc
  being the disc's mean velocity over that revolution (0 while it stands).
  Moving tracks matched to no disc in mover runs are reported, not judged.
* **Cost here is an indication only** (the cloud container, Python,
  per scan); B3 is the Jetson's, on the desktop half (run steps below).

### Amendment 3 (2026-11-28, after B's measurement, before C): the horizon

Written from B2's measured error and `T_REACT_S` (0.25 s), before C is
built or run.

* **Horizon `H = T_REACT_S + REVOLUTION_S = 0.35 s`.** A track is the
  disc's state at its cluster's instant; by the time the veto reads it the
  scan is up to one revolution old (0.1 s), and the robot then needs
  `T_REACT_S` to stop. C projects each moving track from its last
  observation to `H` past the read, as a straight sweep.
* **The swept disc's radius** is the cluster's half-width (B's centre
  estimate) plus the velocity error's p90 times `H`: 0.138 m/s x 0.35 s =
  **4.8 cm** pooled. At 2.5 m/s alone the p90 is 0.87 m/s (30 cm); that
  tail is almost all the disc's instant stop (B's record), where the track
  OVER-predicts motion, so it errs toward a stop, never toward a move. C
  uses the pooled figure and reports C2's time stopped for a predicted
  mover so the cost of the tail is seen.
* **What C cannot expect from B.** A mover is tracked a median 3 scans
  after it comes into view and p90 7 (B2's failed time to first track):
  at 2.5 m/s it covers up to 1.75 m before its first prediction. Until
  then the static veto is all there is, as today. C1 is judged on A3's
  sweep unchanged; this limit is recorded, not a reason to amend C1.

  where the lidar could not see, recedes, or stands, waits for that; and
  revolutions in which the body turned more than 3 degrees are skipped
  (the driver's bins smear them). Widening the free-space test to 0.3-2 s
