# 3.217 The motor board's commands over Wi-Fi while its serial receive is dead (a stopgap)

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.217; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** This Rover's driver board sends its feed on both serial routes
but acts on nothing it receives over either (3.140's log, 2026-11-10 to
11-25). It does obey the same JSON over its web route
(`GET /js?json=<command>`), and on 2026-11-25 that route held 20 Hz from
the Jetson (p50 24 ms, p99 73-116 ms, 6 of 2,600 timed out). The unit is
to be replaced by 12-08; until a board that hears serial is in hand, this
section lets the stack drive this one: **commands go out over HTTP,
feedback still comes in over serial.** Decided by the user, 2026-11-25
("Build it"). It is a stopgap behind a switch; serial stays the default
and the shipped route.

**What it is.**

* `ROBOT_COMMAND_URL` (or `hardware.command_url`), for example
  `http://192.168.86.42`. Unset, `robot/hardware_robot.py` is exactly as
  before.
* Set, every command `_send()` would have written to the port goes to a
  sender thread instead. `set_wheel_velocity()` and `stop()` never wait
  on the network.
* Wheel commands (`T:1`) are one slot, newest wins, so a stop is never
  queued behind older speeds and a stale speed never follows a stop.
  A waiting stop goes first, then set-up commands in order, then a speed
  (as first written, set-up went first: review 1 below).
* A request that does not answer in `COMMAND_TIMEOUT_S` (0.10 s) is
  abandoned, its connection closed, and the newest wheel command sent on
  a new one.
* **No delivery, no motion** (3.34's rule, for the other direction): with
  a command undelivered for `COMMAND_STALE_S` (0.25 s) a non-zero command
  raises `WheelFeedbackLost` and the standing command is zeroed. A zero
  and `stop()` are never refused.
* The board's heartbeat stays 200 ms (3.216) and `robot/safety.py` is not
  touched. Whether 200 ms survives real Wi-Fi is step B's question; if it
  does not, the choice (a longer heartbeat with a longer stop distance, or
  wheels that stutter) goes to the user.
* `sim/fake_esp32.py` gains an HTTP face on the same firmware model, with
  a delay and a fault a test can inject.

**Criteria (written 2026-11-25, before building; CLAUDE.md section 7).**
A is judged on the fake board, commands over its HTTP face, feedback over
its pty.

1. **It drives, and nothing goes down the serial line.** A wheel command
   turns the fake's wheels; the odometry read back over serial is within
   1 cm of the body's truth after a 1 m drive; the pty receives 0 command
   lines.
2. **A stop is not delayed by the network.** With every HTTP reply held
   30 ms, over 200 stops mid-drive: `stop()` returns in under 20 ms every
   time, and the board's setpoint is zero within 150 ms of the call every
   time.
3. **A stale speed never follows a stop.** Over 1,000 seeded
   interleavings of speeds and stops under a 0-40 ms reply delay, the last
   wheel command the board received equals the last one issued, 1,000 of
   1,000.
4. **A dead route refuses motion and never traps a stop.** With the HTTP
   face refusing connections: within 0.35 s (`COMMAND_STALE_S` plus one
   timeout) a non-zero command raises `WheelFeedbackLost` and the standing
   command is zero; `stop()` does not raise; the fake's own heartbeat has
   stopped its wheels within 0.25 s of the last delivered command.
5. **One lost request does not trip the 200 ms heartbeat.** With a single
   reply hung for 1 s mid-drive, the longest gap between `T:1`s the board
   received is under 200 ms and its heartbeat never fired, 20 of 20 runs.
6. **The wheel loop is not held.** With replies held 30 ms,
   `set_wheel_velocity()` p99 under 2 ms over 1,000 calls.
7. **Serial is untouched.** With no command URL the fast tier passes with
   no test changed.
8. **`/health` says which route.** `motor_board` carries the route
   (`serial` or `http`), the age of the oldest undelivered command, and
   counts of sent, failed and coalesced commands: description, never a
   verdict (M5).

Paired progress metric for 2-6 (stability alone rewards a robot that
never moves): criterion 1's 1 m drive.

**Reported, not bars** (the real board, steps B and C): the longest gap
between deliveries over 10 minutes at 20 Hz; whether an HTTP `T:1` feeds
the heartbeat; heartbeat stops per minute of driving.

## Steps

* **A.** The sender, the fake's HTTP face, the tests for 1-8, both specs.
  Simulator only.
* **B.** On the Rover, wheels still: the built sender at 20 Hz for 10
  minutes with zero speeds (`T:1 L0 R0`): gaps and failures. **A `T:1` is
  a motion command, even at zero: the user's go first.**
* **C.** On the Rover, wheels off the floor, the user present: one slow
  command, a stop, and a pulled route (the heartbeat stops it). The
  user's go first.
* **D.** The heartbeat decision from B and C's numbers (the user's).
