# 3.141 The wheel loop late under the full mission stack (3.136.B P4)

*`PLAN-ros-alignment.md` section 3.141; index: [README.md](README.md).*

**Why.** 3.136.B's P4 (the wheel loop under the worst overlap; bar **0 late
ticks** over a 30 min run) FAILED in its first full-stack window on the
Jetson, 2026-11-02 (3.136.B run 1,
`evaluations/jetson-perf-3136/board/run1-20261102-1329/`; the user's rule:
a failed row becomes its own section). This section holds the finding;
nothing is fixed yet.

**The finding (3.136.B run 1, 13:37-14:07, board booted 12:55, 15 W, max
65.5 C, 0 OOM kills, desktop load <= 1.8).** The window: the robot server
in sim mode (fake board, fake D500, `drive: ros`), the ROS container
(`vision-picar-ros:latest`, SLAM + nav2), VE resident as its unit runs it,
YOLOE + CLIP at 4 Hz (`perception_loop.py`), and the brain on back-to-back
tiered find missions (explore searching, the local tier on, `rooms` goal
choice with `find_room_labels: frame`) in `procthor:val:12`; the cloud a $0
stand-in (3.136 amendment 2).

* **4 late wheel ticks in 24,711 moving ticks over 1,800.8 s; `max_dt_s`
  0.1141 s** (the running maximum; 0.0799 s before the window, so a tick in
  this window set it). 3.87's H3 had 0 late in 18,273 (max 99 ms) under
  scripted nav goals with no mission; 3.80.E's `A1-noload` 0 of 12,367.
* When (the 5 s `/health` log, seconds into the window): 486 s (P1:
  perception plus the missions), 992 s (during 3.136's P8 bench: VE asked
  back to back beside the missions' questions), 1,134 s (the perception
  loop restarting with 3.74.C's hazard prompts: its model load), 1,271 s
  (P2: perception with the hazard prompts). Not one event: spread over the
  window, under every load it ran.
* The window was also invalid by 3.136's rule for another reason (10 of 63
  nav2 goals ABORTED: explore's goals in a ProcTHOR house), so 3.136's
  other rows are not scored on it; the late ticks are P4's own bar.

**Open (not decided here):** whether the stall is the event loop (a sync
route or an encode, as 3.87's `/world/map`), CPU contention from 4 Hz
perception and VE, or the sensor programs. 3.87's per-route timing and a
`py-spy` dump at a late tick are the first tools. A late tick is a wheel
step held past 100 ms (two periods) on the loop the safety stop runs on
(3.136's P5 measures that stop's latency).

**The cause, found on the desktop (3.141.A, 2026-11-03).** A stall tracer
in the robot server (`evaluations/wheel-loop-3141/tracer/`: loop gaps, GC
pauses, thread stacks and CPU at a stall, slow vets, slow requests) on
3.136.B's robot-server stack on the desktop (`desktop.py`; a 4 min smoke):

* **The vet itself is the cost on the wheel tick.** `wheel_loop()` calls
  `SafetyController.vet_wheel_velocity()` on the event loop, inside the
  tick, so its time adds to the next tick's `dt` (`last` is taken before
  the body). In the smoke, vets of 5-10 ms on the loop; the slow ones are
  turns. `vet_bench.py` splits it by case on a 360-bin scan (the D500's):
  straight 0.5 ms, a turn in the open 1.8 ms, **a turn clamped near a
  corner 9-16 ms p50, up to 28 ms** on the desktop -- and on the board
  (quiet, 15 W) **20.1-22.8 ms p50** (`board/vet-bench-20261103-1209.txt`;
  `BOARD-BENCH.md`). Why: when the asked turn is blocked, `pivot_scale()`
  runs `pivot_blocked()` 8 times (the asked turn, six halvings, the reason),
  and each pass re-derives every scan point in the body frame and rotates
  it across the five aged-arc samples (3.42), in plain Python. The slower
  turns the search tries mostly pass, so they visit every point. nav2
  rotates in place beside furniture all the time in a ProcTHOR house, so
  this case is common under explore.
* With 50 ms of sleep, ~22 ms of vet leaves ~28 ms for everything else
  before the 100 ms line; the board's pre-window worst tick was 79.9 ms
  (3.136.B run 1), and under the full stack (perception at 4 Hz, VE, the
  missions' threadpool routes) the same vet runs slower. The rarity fits:
  the search runs only while a turn is clamped beside something.
* **Not the cause:** the GC (the desktop's old generation held ~3,000
  objects after 3.37's freeze; a full collection that size is a few ms even
  on the board's Python 3.10), and slow `/world/map` replies (170 of 862
  over 120 ms in run 1, against 4 late ticks; they run in the threadpool).
  The desktop runs Python 3.14 (incremental GC), so the GC half is an
  inference from the heap's size, not a measured board pause; criterion 4's
  traced board run measures it.

**The fix.** `pivot_scale()`'s search does the per-scan work once: the
body-frame points, their aged-arc rotations and each one's distance to the
chassis now are computed once per vet and shared by the eight passes, and a
point farther than `PIVOT_MARGIN_CM + 20` now (which no pass can block on)
is dropped once instead of eight times. Nothing it decides changes: the
same points in the same order, the same arithmetic, the same first blocking
point and reason. `_turn_since_scan()` is read once per search, not once
per pass (on a live body the reading could move between passes; a fixed
body gives the same number to all eight).

   the identical vetted `(left, right, reason)`: **0 differences**. Planted
   bugs (an off-by-one in the arc, a dropped margin point, a reordered

2. **The cost, on the board, quiet (`vet_bench.py`, as above):** the
   corner-turn cases **p90 <= 5 ms** (dev 21.9-22.9 ms), and no other case
   slower than dev's p90 by more than 10%. Desktop numbers recorded beside

3. **The suite** passes unedited (`ready`), and one live mission through
   the desktop stack (`desktop.py`, short window) ends normally with the
   tracer showing no vet over 5 ms.
4. **The board, full stack (P4's bar, 3.136.B's window configuration):**
   **0 late wheel ticks over 30 min**, `max_dt_s` recorded, with the
   tracer in the robot server so any tick over 80 ms is attributed (vet,

**3.141.D, the traced window: the suspect and criteria (2026-11-04,
written before the runs).** 3.141.C's six late ticks came one every 4-5
min, each 101-113 ms, in every load phase. The suspect is 3.37's: a full
(generation-2) collection of the robot server's heap. 3.37's
`gc.freeze()` moves start-up's objects out of the collector's reach, but
everything made after it (requests, scans, the wheel loop's records) ages
into generation 2, so a full pass walks more each time; at 3.37 the pass
was 55-92 ms every ~75 s, and a pause of ~50-60 ms on top of the 50 ms
sleep is what a 101-113 ms tick needs. Other candidates the tracer can name:
a slow call on the loop (a vet, a sync route's encode), or the robot
server starved of CPU (its threads' CPU over the stall).

The tracer rides the window as `steady.sh STEADY_TRACER=1`: its directory
after 3.136's shim on the robot server's `PYTHONPATH` (the shim chains it;
the tracer chains only what comes after it), `TRACE_3141_LOG` in `$OUT`.

1. **Smoke (D1): the tracer costs the robot server nothing that matters.**
   A short window (`WINDOW_S=300`, `P1_S=120`, `P2_S=120`) with the tracer:
   its own threads' CPU plus its probes' work on the event loop (both
   cumulative in each `heap` row; the window's share is the last row's
   minus the first's) **at most 2% of one core** over the window, the 5 ms probe and the
   50 ms shadow probe running throughout (rows present), and **no late
   tick at a moment the tracer's sampler was itself running** (a `stall`
   row whose stacks show only the tracer). (Amendment 1 restates these
   conditions and drops the fallback: the trace needs the sampler.) Failing
   D1, D2 is not run; the tracer is fixed first.
2. **The window (D2): every late tick is named.** 3.136.B's 30 min window
   with the tracer. For each moving tick over 100 ms (`wheel_loop`'s count,
   located by the 50 ms shadow probe's `tick` rows), the trace has a record
   overlapping it: a `gc` row, a `stall` row's stacks and thread CPU, or a
   slow `vet`/`req`. **The suspect is confirmed if at least half of the late
   ticks coincide (within 0.2 s) with a generation-2 collection over 30
   ms**; otherwise the named records are the next cause. The progress half:
   moving ticks and the missions' outcomes, as 3.141.C.

**Amendment 1 to D1-D2 (2026-11-04, before either run; from the tracer's
reviews).** As first written, D1's third condition could not be measured
(a stall row never shows the sampler, and Python 3.10 names no threads), and
D2 located late ticks by the 50 ms shadow probe, which misses up to a third
of them (its phase is not the wheel loop's). Now:
* The tracer writes nothing from the event loop: every row is queued and
  only its sampler thread writes the file (a row written from the loop
  waited on the lock the sampler held while encoding a stall). Each `heap`
  row carries `tracer_tids`, `loop_tid`, `sampler_ticks` and
  `sampler_errors`, and every row its `pid`.
* **D1:** (a) the tracer's threads' CPU plus its probes' measured work, the
  window's last `heap` row minus its first, **at most 2% of one core**;
  (b) `sampler_ticks` rises in every `heap` row of the window and
  `sampler_errors`, `rows_dropped` stay 0; (c) **in no `stall` row do the
  tracer's own threads (`tracer_tids`) use more CPU than the loop thread**.
  Recorded as a limit: asyncio's own per-wake work for the 5 ms probe is not
  in (a).
* **D2:** a late tick is located by the 5 ms probe's `gap` rows (every
  event-loop pause over 20 ms) and `stall` rows; the 50 ms shadow probe
  (now over 60 ms) is a cross-check. The rest of D2 stands: the suspect is
  confirmed if at least half of the window's late ticks (`wheel_loop`'s
  count) are matched by a `gap` of 50 ms or more that a generation-2 `gc`
  row of 30 ms or more overlaps (within 0.2 s). Recorded as limits: a stall
  row's per-thread CPU is from a baseline up to 1 s old at 10 ms ticks, so
  it names CPU starvation only when one thread stands far above its usual
  rate; rows before `window_start` (start-up's own collection) are not
  scored.

**Amendment 2 to D1-D2 (2026-11-04, before either run).** `wheel_loop`
counts late ticks but stamps none, so D2 matches by interval:
`steady.sh` logs the count every 5 s (`wheel.log`), and a late tick in an
interval is **matched** when that interval holds a `gap` of 50 ms or more
that a generation-2 `gc` row of 30 ms or more overlaps (within 0.2 s); the
suspect is confirmed if at least half the window's late ticks are matched,
one to one (a qualifying gap matches at most one late tick in its
interval). A `stall` row's `drain_ms` over 10 ms marks it as possibly the
tracer's own (an encode holds the GIL); such rows never count as a match.
D1(b) also needs a `heap` row at least every 2 x `HEAP_S` (a wedged
sampler writes none), and rows lost to a failed write count in
`rows_dropped`.
